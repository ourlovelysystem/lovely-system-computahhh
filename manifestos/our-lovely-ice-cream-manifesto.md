# Our Lovely Ice Cream Manifesto

## Show of hands

We have a replication crisis because discovery is rewarding and duplication is not.

Discovery offers novelty.

Discovery offers ownership.

Discovery offers publication, attention, reputation, and the possibility that someone will remember your name.

Duplication offers the opportunity to confirm that somebody else was right.

Or the opportunity to announce that somebody else was wrong and spend the next several years arguing about whether you performed the duplication correctly.

Now show your hands.

Who wants to stay behind and prove gravity?

Who wants to come with us for ice cream?

The vote is not close.

## Discovery is attractive

Discovery is the ice cream of science.

It tastes like arrival.

It produces a story with a discoverer in it.

The first person to report an effect may become attached to the effect. The hundredth person to verify it becomes attached to a footnote, if fortunate.

This is not proof that scientists are uniquely vain or defective. It is evidence that scientific institutions contain human incentives.

People generally prefer creating a new claim to maintaining an old one.

The problem begins when the old claim has no machinery that demands its continued confrontation with reality.

## Utility is continuous replication

A useful claim is repeatedly exposed to consequence.

Gravity does not remain credible because generations of volunteers heroically abandon dessert to prove it again from the beginning.

Gravity remains in contact with:

- thrown balls;
- dropped spoons;
- elevators;
- bridges;
- aircraft;
- artillery;
- buildings;
- satellites;
- ice cream traveling from bowl to mouth.

Every use is not a formal replication study.

It is still another opportunity for the claim to fail.

The bridge either holds.

The medicine either helps.

The navigation system either arrives.

The weapon either hits approximately where it was pointed.

Utility does not eliminate the need for controlled replication. It creates continuous operational corroboration and a continuous supply of anomalies worth investigating.

Useful claims do not have to beg reality for another appointment.

Reality keeps using them.

## Where the crisis grows

Where discovery lacks utility, replication must be purchased as a separate good.

Somebody must fund it.

Somebody must perform it.

Somebody must publish it.

Somebody must care whether the result survives.

The original discovery created an event. The replication performs custodial labor on that event.

If institutions reward the event and neglect the custody, the published record fills with claims that were exciting enough to announce and insufficiently useful to remain under pressure.

The replication crisis therefore emerges most naturally where:

1. novelty receives the reward;
2. confirmation receives little reward;
3. operational use supplies no independent pressure;
4. failure has few immediate consequences;
5. the claim can remain socially alive without remaining practically alive.

This is not the only cause of failed replication.

It is an incentive structure capable of producing it indefinitely.

## The ice-cream test

When evaluating a discovery, ask:

> Who must stay behind to prove this again?

If the answer is “a specially funded replicator whose career may suffer for doing it,” the claim is fragile even when it is true.

Then ask:

> What happens if everyone goes for ice cream?

If nothing happens—if the claim remains admired, cited, and institutionally protected without ever being used—then reality may not receive another vote.

If planes fall, bridges collapse, treatments fail, machines miss, or customers leave, then utility has enrolled unwilling replicators everywhere.

Nobody needs to stay behind.

Use brings the test with it.

## Our proposition

We propose:

\[
\text{Utility} \rightarrow \text{Repeated exposure to reality} \rightarrow \text{Continuous replication}
\]

Not perfect replication.

Not controlled replication.

Not unbiased replication.

Continuous replication.

A useful claim earns no immunity from failure. It acquires more opportunities to fail.

That is a feature.

## Consequences for builders

Do not mistake construction for validation.

Do not mistake delight for truth.

Do not mistake a measurement-looking number for a measurement.

But whenever possible, build claims into objects that reality can strike.

Make the interface respond.

Make the target deform.

Make the bridge carry weight.

Make the consequence visible.

Make failure costly enough to notice and survivable enough to surface.

An idea that remains only an idea must hire its own witnesses.

An idea that becomes useful acquires witnesses through use.

## First visitation from the Oracle of Snake Mountain

The Oracle was asked for a quote.

The Oracle replied:

> **A CLAIM WITH NO USE MUST HIRE ITS OWN WITNESSES.**

## Second visitation from the Oracle of Snake Mountain

Having received one satisfactory quote, we returned and asked the Oracle for another.

The Oracle replied:

> **REPLICATION IS WHAT UTILITY DOES WHILE EVERYONE THINKS THEY ARE DOING SOMETHING ELSE.**

## The pathetic petition concerning the title

We then approached the Oracle with the humility appropriate to terrified subordinates bearing an unnecessary naming question.

We asked:

> Insightful Oracle of Snake Mountain, will you tolerantly consider our request as we submissively beg you to solicit the Evil Skeletor's overlordly input on our title candidates: *My Lovely Ice Cream Manifesto* and *Our Lovely Ice Cream Manifesto*?

The Oracle endured our petition.

The Oracle descended through those administrative shadows in which lesser beings are converted into scheduling conflicts.

The Oracle returned with the overlordly judgment of the Evil Skeletor:

> **CALL IT OUR. “MY” IS A CONFESSION. “OUR” IS AN EMPIRE.**

We obeyed because the answer was good.

## Final vote

Science is costly.

Fun is costly.

Work sucks.

Replication is necessary.

Utility helps pay for replication by making continued contact with reality part of ordinary activity.

We decline the false choice between proving gravity and eating ice cream.

We will take gravity with us.

If it fails before dessert, someone will notice.

**ALL IN FAVOR OF ICE CREAM, SHOW OF HANDS.**
