# Event 047 — Generalized Quorum Switch Instrument

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Design published

## Generalization

The two-person publication switch described in Event 046 is recognized as one configuration of a more general control primitive.

Our Lovely System can instrument a process using:

- any defined number of eligible switches or participants;
- a configurable time boundary for recognizing concurrent signals;
- a configurable fractional quorum;
- a configurable approval threshold; and
- a configurable denial threshold.

The physical or user-interface form of the switch is not fundamental to the governance rule.

A switch may be as simple as:

- a button contained in an email;
- a button or control in a web application;
- an authenticated link;
- a mobile control; or
- another mechanism capable of producing an attributable signal.

## Generic rule

The control primitive can be expressed conceptually as:

```text
participants: N
concurrency_window: T
minimum_participation: q
approve_threshold: a
deny_threshold: d
```

The system collects attributable signals from the defined population within the declared time boundary and evaluates them according to the quorum and threshold rules in force for that process.

The essential record is not the appearance of the button. It is:

```text
who acted
what they signaled
when they acted
what process/artifact they acted upon
what decision rule was in force
what result the rule produced
```

## Independent dimensions

The design treats several dimensions as independently configurable rather than hard-coding them into the interface:

**Population** — who is eligible to signal.

**Concurrency window** — how close in time signals must occur to be recognized together.

**Quorum** — what fraction or number of eligible actors must participate before a decision may be recognized.

**Approval threshold** — what fraction or number of relevant signals produces approval.

**Denial threshold** — what fraction or number of relevant signals produces denial or blocks the action.

Additional constraints, such as the apparent geographic-separation rule described in Event 046, may be applied to particular instruments without becoming requirements of every quorum instrument.

## Example configurations

The future two-person publication switch is one narrow configuration:

```text
N = 2
minimum_participation = 1.0
approve_threshold = 1.0
concurrency_window = 5 seconds
additional condition = observed network separation >= X miles
```

A deliberative process could instead use a larger population, a longer recognition window, a fractional quorum, and different approval and denial thresholds.

The same underlying instrument can therefore express substantially different decision processes without requiring a different fundamental control mechanism.

## Transport versus governance

An email button or web-app button is sufficient to instantiate a switch if the resulting signal can be associated with the actor and the relevant decision context.

Accordingly:

> **The button emits the signal. The interpretation layer supplies the governance.**

This separation permits interfaces to remain simple while decision rules remain explicit and inspectable.

## Finding

Our Lovely System can treat quorum-based concurrence as a reusable process instrument rather than as a special-purpose voting application or publication feature.

> **Collect attributable signals from a defined population, within a defined time boundary, then apply a declared quorum and threshold rule.**

The two-person switch is therefore not the architecture. It is a test case and configuration of the architecture.

**Disposition:** GENERALIZED QUORUM SWITCH INSTRUMENT PUBLISHED. POPULATION, CONCURRENCY, QUORUM, APPROVAL, AND DENIAL ARE INDEPENDENTLY CONFIGURABLE. SWITCH TRANSPORT IS SEPARATED FROM GOVERNANCE INTERPRETATION.
