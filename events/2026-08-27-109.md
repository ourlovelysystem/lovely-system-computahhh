## POSITION BY WILL DALY

Will Daly stated:

> I position bullying as an inescapable organizational primitive of "free" action within a bound system. I claim that the legal system is not "closed" to people who have money but it is a "bound" system for people of modest means.

When an initial response equated pressure with bullying, Daly corrected it:

> I have claimed to be a bully. Your response defaults to its normative use. I am using it descriptively.

Daly then supplied a further correction:

> I will not say that pressure is bullying. I will say that pressure can readily be placed under a bullying lens which intimidated actors are apt to interpret as bullying and which sympathetic actors will classify as righteous indignation.

These corrections control the meaning of this entry.

## DISTINCTIONS

Daly's position does not define pressure as bullying.

It distinguishes:

- **The conduct:** pressure applied by one actor to another.
- **The bullying lens:** an interpretation readily applied to that pressure, particularly by an intimidated actor.
- **The righteous-indignation lens:** a competing interpretation likely to be applied by an actor sympathetic to the pressure or its purpose.
- **The normative judgment:** a separate question not resolved merely by selecting either label.

Daly's acceptance of responsibility for “bullying Facebook” therefore does not amount to a confession that pressure is inherently wrongful. It accepts that his conduct can be classified through the bullying lens and places his name behind the act.

## BOUND SYSTEMS

Daly describes “free” action as occurring within systems that impose practical bounds.

The legal system serves as his example. Its procedures may be formally available without being equally traversable. Money changes how much process an actor can sustain: advice, filings, discovery, delay, negotiation, trial, appeal, and the absorption of adverse outcomes all consume resources.

The claim is not that the legal system is literally closed to people of modest means.

The claim is that formal access can coexist with materially unequal bounds. A wealthy actor and a modest actor may enter the same system while possessing radically different ranges of sustainable action inside it.

Within that environment, pressure and its interpretation cannot be separated from power, resources, intimidation, sympathy, and the observer's position.
