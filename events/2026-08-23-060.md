## EVALUATION

The observation is correct.

“Most famous speech” is not a neutral selection rule. Fame is plausibly produced in part by rhetorical force, emotional intensity, repetition, cadence, memorability, historical consequence, and quotability. These are closely related to what the Fibrillator records as signal strength.

Selecting famous speeches therefore preselects artifacts likely to score highly on signal.

This creates selection bias:

1. Four famous speeches were selected.
2. All four produced high average signal scores.
3. Their high signal cannot safely be attributed to heroism or villainy.
4. Fame is a competing explanation introduced by the sampling rule itself.

The hero average signal was **8.35**.

The villain average signal was **8.83**.

The difference was **0.48** on a ten-point interpretive scale.

Given the tiny sample, heuristic scoring, semantic slice boundaries, and fame-based selection, that difference does not support a general claim that villain rhetoric signals more strongly than hero rhetoric.

The information-density difference is larger:

- Hero: **8.25**
- Villain: **6.80**
- Difference: **1.45**

That result is worth investigating, but it also cannot support a broad moral classification from four deliberately famous speeches. It may reflect speech type, translation, excerpt selection, historical setting, the specific speakers chosen, or scoring judgment.

## METHOD CORRECTION

A better test would separate fame from moral classification.

Possible comparison sets include:

- famous heroes versus obscure heroes;
- famous villains versus obscure villains;
- famous speeches versus ordinary speeches by the same speakers;
- matched speeches addressing similar crises, audiences, lengths, and media conditions;
- blinded scoring in which the scorer does not know the speaker or moral category;
- multiple independent scorers;
- acoustic signal scored separately from textual signal;
- information density scored separately from truthfulness.

The next useful question is not merely whether heroes and villains differ.

It is whether fame itself predicts Fibrillator signal strength after other variables are controlled.
