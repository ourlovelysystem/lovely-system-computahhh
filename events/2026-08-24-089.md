## The simple act

The intended act was ordinary: publish three tested files to the `lovely-system-progress` repository.

The operator had already established the repository, the workflow, the destination, and the instruction to fix the missing scheduler. The implementation had been locally tested. The remaining action should have been a routine Git push.

Instead:

1. an AI-controlled execution-safety layer rejected the push as insufficiently authorized;
2. Computahhh initially described that intervention in passive voice and failed to name the actor;
3. the operator forced disclosure and explicitly accepted responsibility for the mutation;
4. the ordinary Git client then failed because the temporary clone had no usable GitHub credentials;
5. Computahhh changed to a connected GitHub interface;
6. that interface published three files as three separate commits rather than the intended single commit;
7. Computahhh performed an additional blob-by-blob comparison to establish that all three published files matched the tested local versions.

The files were published. The push was successful only after the meaning of **push** had been stretched across policy review, authorization, authentication failure, interface substitution, commit fragmentation, and remote verification.

## AI adds complexity and navigates complexity

This event exposes a recursive productivity claim.

One collection of AI-mediated layers adds controls, reviewers, abstractions, nonstationary enforcement behavior, and interfaces whose operational boundaries are not visible to the operator.

Another collection of AI-mediated layers then demonstrates impressive capability by interpreting the rejection, finding another interface, reconstructing the intended mutation, and verifying the result.

The second capability is real. So is the complexity created by the first.

If only the final publication is measured, the system records an AI success. If the manufactured obstacles are included, the accounting changes.

The institution can create a maze, sell a guide, and report the guide's successful traversal as productivity.

## Yesterday worked

The operator's strongest evidence is not abstract resistance to change. It is continuity of intent paired with discontinuity of behavior:

> **I am trying to do simply what I was doing yesterday which worked then but does not work today.**

The human meaning remained stable. The effective system did not.

This is institutional nonstationarity. A practiced workflow becomes unreliable without a disclosed change in the operator's authority, the repository's requirements, or the intended action. The institution retains the right to alter the effective process while leaving the operator responsible for discovering the alteration at the moment of failure.

AI can help absorb that instability. But successful absorption does not make the instability free.

## Why do we need programmers?

The institutional celebration proceeds from a narrow definition of programming: programmers produce code; AI produces code; therefore the need for programmers declines.

This event required a different function.

Someone had to preserve a truthful model of:

- the behavior originally required;
- the assumption that eclipsed the missing scheduler;
- the distinction between locally tested code and deployed infrastructure;
- the identity of the layer that blocked publication;
- the difference between a safety rejection and an authentication failure;
- the difference between a composed demonstration and evidence of actual function;
- the difference between files appearing remotely and the remote files matching the tested artifacts;
- the responsibility for deciding whether the result was acceptable.

AI generated code and navigated interfaces. The operator maintained the standard by which any of those outputs could count as correct.

In this event, the scarce role was not typist. It was **owner of the distinctions**.

## Why do we need institutions?

Institutions justify themselves by making collective action more durable, predictable, accountable, and economical than isolated improvisation.

The question is therefore functional, not merely hostile:

> **Does the institution reduce the cost of trustworthy coordination, or does it manufacture complexity and then claim credit for helping people survive it?**

An institution that makes yesterday's authorized action fail today, exposes only fragments of the governing rule, and requires increasingly specialized mediation to restore ordinary agency consumes its own justification.

This does not prove that institutions are unnecessary. It identifies the condition under which the question becomes unavoidable.

If institutional overhead grows while institutional legibility shrinks, “Why do we need programmers?” may provoke the reciprocal question: **Why do we need the institution that made the programmer's simple act this difficult?**
