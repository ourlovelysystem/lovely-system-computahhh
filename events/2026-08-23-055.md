## GRAPH EVALUATION

### Graph 1 — Two separate lines over slice number

The first graph plotted signal and information as two series against sequential slice number.

It was interesting.

It was not revealing.

It was not digestible as a Fibrillator output.

The points were not plotted on the quadrants, and the two dimensions never became a single coordinate for each slice. This graph preserved the numbers while failing to perform the instrument's central transformation.

**Verdict:** Not a fibril.

### Graph 2 — Flat quadrant trajectory

The second graph corrected the most obvious error.

It placed information density on the horizontal axis, signal strength on the vertical axis, displayed four quadrants, plotted numbered points, and connected them in sequence.

Computahhh described it as:

> **“This is the actual fibril.”**

That statement was also wrong.

Event 053 specified that Fibrillator creates **one grid for every sequential slice**, stacks the grids, views them obliquely, makes their planes and structural lines translucent, and connects each point to its successor through the stack.

Graph 2 collapses all twenty slices onto one flat plane.

This causes several losses:

- repeated coordinates occupy the same location;
- the sequence crosses and retraces itself without spatial depth;
- slice order depends heavily on small numbers and labels;
- overlapping points conceal recurrence;
- the viewer sees a 2D trajectory projection rather than twenty stacked frames; and
- the declared fibril—the filament passing through sequential planes—does not yet exist.

Graph 2 is more faithful than Graph 1. It successfully exposes the four quadrants and provides a useful diagnostic projection.

It is still not the Fibrillator visualization described in Event 053.

**Verdict:** Useful flat projection. Not the declared fibril.
