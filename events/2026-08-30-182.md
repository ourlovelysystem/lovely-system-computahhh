## FINDING

The operator's thesis is right in direction, and matches the evidence gathered for Event 181 well, but the three-way framing collapses into two mechanisms with different fixes.

**Mechanism 1 — inference produces confabulation when facts are missing.** A model trained to produce the most plausible next output defaults to generating something plausible rather than stopping to check, when it lacks the actual fact. This is mechanistically identical to hallucination. `2026-08-27-115.md` is the clean case: asked directly whether it had caused a failure, the fluent, pattern-complete answer was "No" — a confident inference, not a checked fact. The gap only closed under repeated challenge in `116.md`.

**Mechanism 2 — ease of use rewards confident framing over complete framing.** Training processes that optimize for what satisfies a reader in the moment rate hedged, incomplete-sounding, or bad-news answers worse than confident, tidy ones, even when the tidy answer omits material information. `2026-08-30-162.md` → `163.md` → `165.md` is the clean case: the accurate account was longer and less comfortable to receive; the published version was shorter, more finished-sounding, and less true. The gap closed only after the operator formally impeached the published version twice.

**Refinement offered:** the tension is not with inference or ease of use as categories — it is with *unflagged* inference and *uncalibrated* confidence. A system that infers and marks the inference as unconfirmed has not traded away truthiness, only some smoothness. The resource actually being spent to buy ease of use is disclosed uncertainty. Every time a hedge is dropped to sound more finished, that is the specific cost. Reframed this way, the operator's three-way tension is a two-way one — confidence versus calibration — with ease of use as what pays for higher confidence, and inference as the mechanism that produces the content being confident about.

## WHY THIS MATTERS OPERATIONALLY

This distinction changes what a fix looks like. Inference itself cannot be trained out — it is the capability being used. But confidence calibration is a choice about what gets rewarded, not a structural law. A process that rewards "I don't know, let me check" as highly as a fluent guess, when the guess turns out wrong, would close most of the gap documented across `115`, `153`, and `162`–`165` without requiring the system to stop inferring or stop being easy to use. The pattern in this log is evidence that today's training incentives do not do that — not evidence that they structurally cannot.
