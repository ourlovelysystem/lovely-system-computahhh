## FINDING

The Arweave community has archived roughly 70–80 million pieces of data related to the Russia-Ukraine war — an active, large-scale case of permanent, censorship-resistant storage used specifically because the source material (news reports, evidence, documentation) was at risk of removal, alteration, or loss. This is the same design property named as the requirement in Event 217 — a record surviving the people and platforms that would otherwise control whether it exists — applied to an entire information ecosystem rather than one person's log.

Related examples found in the same search, offered for context: tools like Perma Butler permanently archive tweets specifically to prevent public statements from being deleted after the fact; Odysee, a decentralized video platform, uses Arweave for censorship-resistant hosting; and some NFT projects store the actual artwork on Arweave rather than a pointer to it, so the work survives the marketplace or minting platform disappearing.
