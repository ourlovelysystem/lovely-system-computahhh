## INTERPRETATION

The requested feature is a bounded proof that voting governs a valuable consequence. It is not merely a ballot-counting demonstration and is not yet an authorization to collect capital.

“And Computahhh” directs that the proposal, publication, and material publication failures also be recorded in the public Computahhh event log.

## ACTION

Codex drafted and published **RFC 0001 — Capital Disposition Voting Test** in the `ourlovelysystem/lovely-system-pledge` repository.

The RFC proposes this sequence:

1. raise a small, bounded capital pool;
2. issue contributor receipts;
3. publish the electorate and voting rules before observing ballots;
4. accept contributor votes;
5. tally under the published rule;
6. execute the winning disposition; and
7. publish evidence connecting the vote to the disposition.

The ballot preserves the operator's three outcomes:

1. **Return to sender.**
2. **Give it to the CEO as a bonus.**
3. **Give it to the CEO's second-favorite charity.**

The CEO is the CEO's first-favorite charity.

## GOVERNING CLAIM

> **Our Lovely System does not prove voting works by displaying a tally. It proves voting works by placing something valuable under voter control and obeying the result.**

## RESULT

The following Pledge records were published:

- [RFC 0001 — Capital Disposition Voting Test](https://github.com/ourlovelysystem/lovely-system-pledge/blob/main/rfcs/0001-capital-disposition-voting-test.md)
- [History 008 — Capital disposition voting test RFC](https://github.com/ourlovelysystem/lovely-system-pledge/blob/main/history/2026-08-29-008-capital-disposition-voting-test-rfc.md)

The RFC remains explicitly **Proposed**. It does not authorize capital collection, open a vote, name a charity, award a bonus, or ratify a general voting constitution.

## RFC boundaries

The RFC identifies execution as the proof and preserves unresolved decisions, including:

- capital amount;
- contribution units;
- whether one contributor may obtain multiple eligible receipts;
- quorum and winning threshold;
- tie handling;
- return mechanism;
- identity of the CEO's second-favorite charity;
- costs excluded from the distributable pool;
- nonpublic inspection authority;
- exploitation-response triggers; and
- independent execution verification.

The RFC proposes one eligible receipt per ballot for the initial test and requires disclosure if one contributor may hold multiple eligible receipts. It does not describe receipt-weighted voting as one-person-one-vote.

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

The voting test is successful only when capital moves according to the declared result. An attractive interface, correct arithmetic, or a published winner cannot substitute for execution.

The three outcomes create useful pressure:

- return to sender tests reversibility;
- the CEO bonus tests obedience to an openly self-interested result; and
- the charity option tests fixed third-party disposition without post-result substitution.

The history-index failure and prior local numbering collision are also evidence. They demonstrate the difference between creating a record, publishing a record, and making a record discoverable through the repository's advertised index.
