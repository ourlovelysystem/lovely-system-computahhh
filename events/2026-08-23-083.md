## What passed and what failed

The Event 082 artifact remains valid evidence of the narrow automated transport-and-convergence test. Four independent clients converged at 36 shots and sequence 36. Its trace and hashes remain valid.

It failed as an intelligible demonstration.

Shots were separated by approximately 220 milliseconds, yielding about 4.5 shots per second. At that cadence, several impacts appeared within a single second. A viewer unfamiliar with the test could not comfortably observe:

- which pane was firing;
- how a volley developed;
- how the path moved from outside the reticle into it and back out;
- how local provenance differed from remote consequence;
- how each quadrant accumulated a distinct pattern.

The artifact made the system busy before it made the behavior legible. Proof that requires prior investment from the viewer is poor demonstration material.

## Remedy

Automation is retained. The remedy does not fabricate human input or describe the scripted run as human play.

The fixed 220-millisecond interval was replaced with a declared irregular cadence:

- 650 ms;
- 780 ms;
- 710 ms;
- 860 ms;
- 690 ms;
- 800 ms;
- 740 ms;
- 900 ms between successive shots in each nine-shot volley.

This averages approximately 766 milliseconds between shots, or about 1.3 shots per second. The four-second inter-volley hold remains unchanged.

The purpose is not to counterfeit human randomness. The purpose is to give each impact enough visual time for the direction and pattern to unfold while preserving deterministic reproducibility and an honest automated-test label.

## Evaluation

The objection does not impeach the prior functional result. It impeaches the inference that a valid test artifact is automatically a useful public demonstration.

Evidence and demonstration overlap, but they optimize for different audiences. Evidence must preserve what happened. Demonstration must also allow a viewer to perceive what happened without already understanding the machinery. The next run must satisfy both.
