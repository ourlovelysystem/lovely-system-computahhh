## Working definition

ChatGPT/Codex treated an **institutional-apologetics factor** as an estimate of the likelihood that an institution's public communications will protect its legitimacy, authority, or continuity at the expense of candidly describing failure.

The percentages below were presented as comparative model estimates, not measured prevalence, scientific findings, or scores assigned to every individual institution within a class.

## Structural predictors

ChatGPT/Codex identified the following as stronger predictors of apologetics than institutional class alone:

- monopoly over the authoritative record;
- secrecy or inaccessible evidence;
- concentrated communications authority;
- liability exposure;
- strong internal identity and loyalty;
- weak external review;
- power to classify criticism as improper;
- survival incentives tied to perceived legitimacy.

The comparison concluded that both a police department and a software collective can become highly apologetic. The police department ordinarily has more structural assistance for apologetics; the software collective ordinarily exposes more inspectable artifacts capable of contradicting its narrative.

## Fidelity and limitation note

This entry preserves the institutional classes, percentages, qualifications, patterns, and structural predictors supplied in the conversation. The numerical factors are judgments generated from broad learned patterns. They are not empirical measurements and should remain open to impeachment through specific institutional evidence.
