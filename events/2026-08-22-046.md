# Event 046 — Two-Person Switch Design

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Design published

## Purpose

Future post messages will use a deliberately narrow publication control surface.

The design is intended to make publication dependent upon recognizable human authorization while preserving an important distinction between **technical proof** and **evidence of intent**.

## Post-message defaults

Future post messages will use the following default safety rules, subject to deliberate revision:

- **No attachments.**
- **No tags.**
- **No comments.**
- **The message and body stand alone.**
- **Publication occurs only after an authorized button press.**
- **If nobody is in a position to press the button, the publication process does not operate.**

The system may prepare, validate, queue, and wait. It may not complete the consequential act merely because automation is available.

## Two-person switch

A later publication control will require two recognizable people to authorize the same publication.

The intended conditions are:

1. Two distinct recognized actors provide authorization.
2. Each actor presses an authorization control.
3. The two presses occur within **five seconds** of one another.
4. The system observes the source IP address associated with each authorization.
5. IP-derived location information is used to estimate the apparent location of each actor.
6. The two observed locations must be separated by at least **X miles**, where X remains to be selected.
7. If the concurrence, identity, timing, or apparent-distance conditions are not satisfied, publication does not occur.

## Known weakness

IP-derived location is not proof of physical location.

VPNs, proxies, network architecture, inaccurate geolocation data, compromised accounts or devices, and deliberate manipulation can defeat or confuse the geographic test.

The system therefore must not record the inference:

> **These two humans were physically X miles apart.**

It may record an observation such as:

> **The system observed authorization signals associated with network locations estimated to be X miles apart.**

## Security objective

The principal value of the control is not that it is impossible to defeat.

The intended property is:

> **The control cannot plausibly be defeated by mistake.**

A successful deliberate circumvention may be technically possible. But manufacturing two apparently independent, sufficiently separated, near-simultaneous authorization events requires conduct qualitatively different from an ordinary accidental button press.

The control therefore establishes an **intent threshold**.

A person cannot plausibly explain a deliberately manufactured second authorization, coordinated timing, impersonation, credential compromise, or manipulated apparent geographic separation as the ordinary consequence of accidentally pressing a publication button.

## Forced statement of intent

The geographic test is consequently better understood as a forced declaration mechanism than as strong proof of location.

An honest successful authorization effectively asserts through observable conduct:

> **I am here, another authorized person is there, and we independently authorize this act now.**

Someone deliberately defeating the mechanism must cause the system to observe evidence representing substantially the same assertion even though the represented concurrence or separation did not occur as advertised.

The technical barrier is defeatable.

The deliberate statement of intent required to defeat it is the useful property.

## Instrumentation

The publication record should preserve observations sufficient for later inspection, potentially including:

```text
press_1:
  actor_ref
  timestamp
  source_ip
  geolocation_result

press_2:
  actor_ref
  timestamp
  source_ip
  geolocation_result

observed_time_delta
observed_distance
decision: ACCEPT | REJECT
publication_ref
```

The record should distinguish observations from conclusions.

## Finding

This control is not designed to make wrongdoing impossible.

It is designed to help distinguish:

> **error from disagreement, disagreement from refusal, refusal from circumvention, and circumvention from accident.**

Its strongest property is semantic rather than cryptographic:

> **The control may be deliberately defeated, but there is no plausible ordinary path by which its complete authorization condition is defeated by mistake.**

**Disposition:** TWO-PERSON SWITCH DESIGN PUBLISHED. DEFAULT PUBLICATION REQUIRES HUMAN ACTION. FUTURE DUAL CONTROL WILL REQUIRE DISTINCT ACTORS, NEAR-SIMULTANEOUS AUTHORIZATION, AND OBSERVED NETWORK SEPARATION. DEFEATABILITY IS ACKNOWLEDGED; ACCIDENTAL DEFEAT IS NOT AN INTENDED PLAUSIBLE FAILURE MODE.
