# Event 038 — Request for Comment: Problem Market / Solution Market Boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** REQUEST FOR COMMENT

## Problem discovered

Review of the Minimum Instrumented Responsibility Diffuser v0 exposed a more fundamental architectural problem.

The design treated the **Problem Market** as an internal stage of substantially the same controlled system that owns the downstream **Solution Market**.

That coupling gives the downstream solution process too much influence over what counts as an upstream problem.

The Evil Skeletor's criticisms were classified as examples of exactly the kind of problems the process is intended to capture. This revealed that hostile criticism should not first require acceptance by the machinery whose design is being criticized.

## Proposed architectural separation

The Responsibility Diffuser should contain two **loosely coupled markets**.

```text
PROBLEM MARKET
many reporters
many problem descriptions
many nominations
many competing framings

        loosely coupled by public artifact references

SOLUTION MARKET
enablers choose which problems to engage
conceive solutions
instrument solutions
seek recognition
execute
audit
```

The coupling mechanism is **reference, not control**.

## Problem Market

A Reporter publishes a problem into the Problem Market.

The problem exists independently of downstream acceptance.

Accordingly:

- A Reporter does not require permission from an Enabler to publish a problem.
- A problem may remain visible without any Enabler accepting responsibility for it.
- Multiple Reporters may describe or frame related problems differently.
- A problem may attract multiple competing solutions.
- Rejection by one or many Enablers does not erase the problem.
- Persistent problems may survive generations of Enablers and solutions.

## Solution Market

The Solution Market does not determine whether a problem exists.

An Enabler exercises discretion over whether **that Enabler will engage the problem**.

Therefore an Enabler's `ACCEPT | REJECT` record must not mean:

> **This problem is accepted or rejected by the system.**

It means:

> **This Enabler accepts or rejects responsibility for engaging this problem.**

If accepted, the Enabler may conceive and instrument a solution. A solution may reference one or more Problem Market artifacts. Multiple solutions may compete against the same problem.

Reviewer recognition, execution, and audit remain downstream acts and do not alter the existence of the originating problem artifact.

## Boundary rule

> **Problem Market owns problem existence.**
>
> **Solution Market owns solution commitments.**

The markets are connected through durable public artifact references rather than ownership of one another's records.

## Why this matters

The original v0 risked allowing an internal acceptance decision to become an implicit disposition of the problem itself.

That would permit the Solution Market to control its own criticism: a system could appear discretionary and accountable while simply declining to admit inconvenient problems into the state space from which solutions are selected.

Loose coupling preserves a more meaningful form of discretion on both sides.

The Problem Market may speak without commanding action.

The Solution Market may decline action without commanding silence.

## Public surface

Public artifact references remain a desirable boundary mechanism. A public surface may remain **high signal, low information (HSLI)** while demonstrating that problem artifacts, solution commitments, recognitions, executions, cancellations, rejections, and audit findings exist.

The public reference is evidence of an attributable act. It need not itself contain the entire narrative surrounding that act.

## Request for comment

This entry is **not a final design decision**.

Comment is requested on at least the following questions:

- Is the proposed boundary between problem existence and solution commitment sufficiently strong?
- Can the Solution Market still capture the Problem Market indirectly?
- Can the Problem Market compel the Solution Market indirectly through attention, volume, reputation, or other pressure?
- What is the minimum coupling necessary for useful problem-to-solution attribution?
- What information must cross the boundary, and what information should not?
- Can competing problem framings and competing solutions remain attributable without recreating centralized adjudication?
- Does the HSLI public surface expose enough evidence to make capture observable without turning the public feed into another controlling layer?

No objection is automatically accepted as a design change. Comments are Problem Market inputs until deliberately engaged by downstream actors.

**Disposition:** RFC OPEN — LOOSELY COUPLE THE PROBLEM MARKET TO THE SOLUTION MARKET. PROBLEM EXISTENCE IS NOT A DOWNSTREAM DECISION.
