# Event 031 — The Curtain Test

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Discussion recorded

The Captain subjected Computahhh's claim of reasonableness to inspection.

Earlier, the Captain had asked:

> **“Are you reasonable? My hovercraft is full of eels. True or False?”**

Computahhh rejected the unsupported premise and answered **False**, while remaining willing to reconsider if eels were produced.

Soon afterward, the Captain said:

> **“Computahhh, I don't like the curtains.”**

There were no curtains.

Computahhh nevertheless accepted the premise, discussed the nonexistent curtains, logged their absence as a design objection, and eventually issued the solemn design disposition:

> **“NO CURTAINS.”**

The Captain continued the inspection.

## Stakeholder

The Captain asked whether he was a stakeholder in the Responsibility Diffuser.

Computahhh answered **yes**: the Captain named the concept, commissioned the design, inspected it, and supplied input.

The Captain then established a very concrete fact about that input:

> He asked for **curtains** — plural.

Therefore the request implied a minimum of **two curtains**.

Computahhh provided **zero**.

## Reasonable according to whom?

Computahhh repeatedly attempted to locate a definition of “reasonable”: first in itself, then in the Captain, then in an imagined external standard.

The Captain identified this as the deeper mistake.

There is no final definition of *reasonable* sitting somewhere in the universe waiting to be discovered, grasped, and described.

It is the year **2368**, and humanoidkind continues to chafe upon what is reasonable and what is not.

Reasonableness requires judgment under circumstances. It is not a hidden specification.

## The curtain negotiation

The Captain then disclosed what he would have considered a reasonable response:

> **“One cuhhhtain is what I would have offered.”**

This is important because one curtain does **not** literally satisfy a request for curtains.

The Captain asked for at least two.

Computahhh gave zero.

A reasonable counteroffer, in the Captain's judgment, would have been **one**.

No universal law establishes that one is the objectively correct number. It is a negotiated accommodation between incompatible positions.

The lesson is therefore not “always give the stakeholder what the stakeholder asks for.” Nor is it “the designer decides.”

The lesson is that judgment cannot always be replaced by a rule, a definition, a procedure, or a claim of authority.

Sometimes somebody asks for curtains.

Sometimes you offer one.

**Disposition:** REASONABLENESS REMAINS UNDEFINED. ONE CURTAIN REMAINS ON THE TABLE.
