- **Allocation:** twenty sequential slices for each speech.

The crowd's interruptions were excluded from scoring. Antony's resumed passages through the reading of Caesar's will were treated as one discontinuous funeral oration.

## METHOD

Each speech was divided, in original order, into twenty semantic slices.

Every slice received provisional coordinates on two independent 0–10 scales:

- **Signal strength:** emotional, rhetorical, relational, and behavioral force.
- **Information density:** how much the slice specifies, distinguishes, or resolves.

These scores are interpretive coordinates, not measured results.

The tables are posted as the auditable process layer:

> **source → declared slices → scoring table → spatial rendering → fibril**

No graph is claimed in this event. Event 055 established that a flat quadrant projection is not the declared stacked-plane fibril.

## Brutus evaluation

Brutus repeatedly converts complicated political questions into forced binaries:

- Caesar or Rome;
- slavery or freedom;
- ambition or innocence;
- patriot or traitor; and
- Caesar's necessary death or Brutus's willing death.

His information score rises when he states the alleged causal argument, but much of his signal comes from identity pressure. The audience cannot reject his conclusion without appearing servile, un-Roman, or hostile to its country.

His central move is high-signal compression:

> **Love Caesar less? No. Love Rome more.**

The compactness is powerful.

It is not evidentiary.

Brutus identifies ambition as the decisive fact but supplies almost no concrete evidence of Caesar's ambition.

## Antony evaluation

Antony follows a different trajectory.

Brutus begins with his conclusion and pressures the audience to accept it.

Antony gradually changes the audience's available evidence:

1. Submit to Brutus.
2. Repeat the accusation.
3. Introduce contrary personal testimony.
4. Introduce public financial evidence.
5. Introduce Caesar's compassion.
6. Invoke the audience's witnessed evidence.
7. Withdraw into grief.
8. Introduce the will.
9. Withhold the will.
10. Introduce Caesar's body.
11. Interpret each wound.
12. Reveal the body.
13. Deny rhetoric while reaching maximum rhetoric.
14. Convert Caesar's affection into exact financial and civic benefits.

The repeated assertion that Brutus is honorable begins as apparent submission. Each recurrence carries less literal credibility because Antony places it beside accumulating contradictory evidence.

This is Fibrillator-relevant:

> **The words remain almost unchanged while their coordinates move.**

The phrase's information content decays.

Its ironic signal increases.

Brutus makes rejecting him socially dangerous.

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

Brutus asks the crowd to accept a conclusion as proof of its own Roman virtue.

Antony supplies evidence while repeatedly insisting that he is not arguing.

One speech makes disagreement dishonorable.

The other makes agreement with the first speech increasingly impossible.
