# Event 036 — Minimum Instrumented Responsibility Diffuser v0

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Conceptual instrument submitted for line-item veto

The Captain supplied the essential minimum roles for an instrumented Responsibility Diffuser and reserved line-item veto authority.

## Captain's essential minimum

### 0. Reporter
Sees problems. Describes problems. Publishes problem summaries. Nominates some problems as opportunities.

### 1. Enabler
Has discretion. Learns about problems. Accepts some. Rejects others. Maintains credibility by logging tasks accepted and tasks rejected. Low rejections signal capture. Conceives solutions. Instruments solutions.

### 2. Reviewer
Has discretion. Responsible for recognizing the action as instrumented as it applies to the problem as conceived.

### 3. Auditor
Has discretion. Responsible for surfacing and reporting issues after the fact. Every instance of every action executed is visible by reference to an auditor through public-facing information feeds.

Artifact references are public-facing.

A public surface is highly desirable. The public surface may be **high signal, low information (HSLI)**. The Captain supplied cancellation numbers as an example.

## Computahhh's minimum instrument

The proposed unit of responsibility is an **attributable act**, rather than a person, role, problem, or project.

The four roles establish the kinds of acts that create responsibility.

| Role | Essential act | Public artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reporter | Publish a problem summary; optionally nominate it as an opportunity | Problem reference |
| Enabler | Accept or reject; if accepted, conceive and instrument a solution | Decision reference + solution reference |
| Reviewer | Recognize whether the instrumented action addresses the problem as conceived | Recognition reference |
| Auditor | After execution, surface/report issues and make every executed action publicly referable | Execution/audit reference |

Each consequential act produces a durable reference. References connect forward and backward.

Conceptual path:

```text
PROBLEM P-17
    |
    +-- reported by --> Reporter
    |
    +-- nominated opportunity
              |
              v
DECISION D-08
    |
    +-- accepted/rejected by --> Enabler
    |
    +-- if accepted, instruments
              v
SOLUTION S-04
    |
    +-- recognized by --> Reviewer
              v
ACTION A-219
    |
    +-- execution instance
    |
    +-- visible through --> Auditor
              v
AUDIT U-219
```

## Minimum record types

Six record types were proposed:

```text
PROBLEM
  ref
  reporter
  summary
  opportunity: yes/no

DECISION
  ref
  problem_ref
  enabler
  disposition: ACCEPT | REJECT
  reason

SOLUTION
  ref
  decision_ref
  enabler
  description

RECOGNITION
  ref
  solution_ref
  problem_ref
  reviewer
  disposition: RECOGNIZED | NOT_RECOGNIZED

EXECUTION
  ref
  solution_ref
  instance
  timestamp

AUDIT
  ref
  execution_ref
  auditor
  issue_ref: optional
```

Rejected decisions are first-class artifacts. Otherwise Enabler discretion becomes invisible whenever the answer is **no**.

The Captain's observation that low rejection rates may signal capture is therefore instrumentable. No mandatory rejection ratio is proposed: low rejection is a signal, not proof of capture.

## Public HSLI surface

The public feed need not explain everything in order to expose consequential activity.

Example:

```text
P-17 -> ACCEPTED
S-04 -> RECOGNIZED
A-219 -> EXECUTED
U-219 -> NO ISSUE REPORTED

CANCELLATIONS: 37
REJECTIONS: 14
NON-RECOGNITIONS: 3
AUDIT ISSUES: 8
```

The design distinguishes **public visibility** from **public explanation**.

The public is owed evidence that consequential acts occurred and durable references to those acts. Additional information may exist behind those references where appropriate.

## Minimal responsibility path

```text
SEE
 Reporter

   v

DECIDE
 Enabler

   v

RECOGNIZE
 Reviewer

   v

DO
 Action

   v

OBSERVE
 Auditor

   loop
```

No role automatically commands the next.

- Reporter cannot compel acceptance.
- Enabler cannot compel recognition.
- Reviewer does not execute merely by recognizing.
- Auditor cannot retroactively make the action correct merely by recording it.
- Every transition leaves evidence.

Responsibility accumulates rather than simply transferring. Later participation does not erase earlier attributable acts.
