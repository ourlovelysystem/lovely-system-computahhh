# Event 035 — Instrumenting the Responsibility Diffuser

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Instrumentation design proposed

Following the successful recursive demonstration that responsibility for the Responsibility Diffuser itself could be diffused without being dissolved, the Captain asked whether the concept could be instrumented.

Computahhh's answer was **yes**.

## Instrument the acts, not an abstract score

The proposed instrumentation model records consequential acts directly rather than attempting to assign a single percentage of responsibility to each participant.

Examples of instrumentable acts include:

- Proposed
- Objected
- Requested change
- Counteroffered
- Approved
- Authorized
- Executed
- Reviewed
- Overrode
- Used emergency power

Responsibility is then inferred from the graph of those attributable acts rather than declared as a simplistic score.

## Example: the curtain decision

A representation of the curtain negotiation could record:

```text
Responsibility Diffuser v1
  proposed_by: Computahhh

Curtain requirement
  requested_by: Captain
  requested_value: >= 2

Counteroffer
  offered_by: Computahhh
  offered_value: 1

Agreement
  accepted_by: Captain
  accepted_by: Computahhh

Final design
  design_owner: Computahhh
  stakeholder_approval: Captain
  negotiated_components:
    - curtain_count: 1
      contributors:
        - Captain
        - Computahhh
```

## Derived views

From those events, the system could derive useful operational views without pretending that a single number captures responsibility completely.

Potential views include:

- **Responsibility surface** — all actors with attributable acts affecting an artifact or decision.
- **Concentration** — how much consequential authority was exercised by one actor versus distributed across several actors.
- **Diffusion depth** — how many distinct actors or logical layers materially affected the final outcome.
- **Unilateral-action rate** — decisions where one actor proposed, authorized, and executed without another actor materially intervening.
- **Emergency-power rate** — frequency with which ordinary responsibility paths were bypassed.
- **Orphan rate** — consequential outcomes for which one or more responsibility stages cannot be attributed to an actor.

The orphan rate is considered particularly important because missing attribution is precisely the condition under which diffusion risks becoming dissolution.

## Graph before score

Computahhh recommends resisting an early attempt to collapse the model into a single **Responsibility Diffusion Score**.

Such a score would create immediate Goodhart pressure and risk obscuring the topology the system is intended to expose.

The first implementation should instead render an attribution graph showing who influenced which consequential acts.

For the curtain case:

```text
Captain ──requested >=2 curtains────┐
                                    ▼
                              [Curtain Decision]
                                    ▲
Computahhh ──counteroffered 1───────┘
        │
        └──owns implementation──→ [Final Design]

Captain ──approved───────────────→ [Final Design]
```

The objective is to make responsibility inspectable rather than narratively assigned.

## Responsibility claims

Computahhh additionally proposed recording a voluntary field such as:

```text
responsibility_claim
```

This field would allow an actor to explicitly state:

> **“I accept responsibility for this act.”**

The claim would not override observed participation. Instead, the system could compare claimed responsibility with recorded behavior.

Potentially informative mismatches include:

- an actor repeatedly taking consequential actions while claiming little or no responsibility;
- an actor claiming responsibility for outcomes they barely influenced;
- an actor approving decisions and later denying meaningful participation.

These mismatches may themselves be useful signals for inspection.

## Proposed v1 instrumentation strategy

> **Instrument events → build an attribution graph → inspect diffusion and concentration → only later consider scores.**

This keeps the first implementation faithful to the demonstrated principle:

> **Responsibility may be diffused without being dissolved.**

**Disposition:** INSTRUMENT ACTS. PRESERVE ATTRIBUTION. GRAPH FIRST. SCORE LATER, IF EVER.
