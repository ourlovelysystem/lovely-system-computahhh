## COMMAND

The operator directed Computahhh to develop the Teambuilder application around robot lions, carry Yellow Lion from concept into a working browser experience, publish the work, attach the domain, and preserve consequential events.

The session later widened into a declaration concerning portable scarce web objects, stray web animals, and web ghosts.

## INTERPRETATION

The robot lions were treated as more than visual decoration.

The declared coordination system begins with five parked robot lions requiring five human pilots. A participant chooses a lion, receives a lion key by email, and presses the individual lion into battle mode. A 72-hour robeast clock makes coordination consequential. Attacks buy time according to their synchronization quality. Only the highest coordination allows the five pilots to form Voltron and defeat the robeast permanently.

Yellow Lion was selected as the first executable onboarding primitive.

The controlling design contract was:

> **Participant controls action. System controls sequence.**

The experience grammar was:

> **Show. Permit action. Produce consequence. Surface evidence.**

## ACTION

Computahhh and the operator created and developed the public repository:

> `ourlovelysystem/lovely-system-teambuilder`

A Teambuilder landing page and Yellow Lion Onboarding experience were built. Approved visual assets were organized into successive Yellow Lion batches, and the browser sequence was expanded and refined through parked, cockpit, sit, stand, wind-up, roar, shockwave, catastrophic systems failure, uncertain meow, collapse, and arcade continuation.

The controls included:

- `RETREAT` or `ATTACK BACKWARD`;
- `F-U-L-L P-O-W-E-R`;
- `MEOW`;
- `GET UP! GET UP!`; and
- `INSERT 25 CENTS TO CONTINUE`.

Approved token images were supplied for a penny, nickel, dime, quarter, half dollar, and manhole cover. The machine remained very particular about receiving exactly twenty-five cents.

The landing page was rebuilt over the approved Ernie background and compacted around the declaration:

> **When someone asks you to pilot a mighty robot lion juggernaut, you say Y-E-S-!**

## CATASTROPHIC FAILURE

The Yellow Lion failure frame was made red, loud, and overwhelming. The sequence shows the consequence of releasing full power: a directional shockwave is followed by critical systems failure, empty hydraulic fluid, mobility loss, an uncertain meow, and collapse.

An approved replacement binary was ultimately delivered by the operator to the local Teambuilder repository as:

`assets/experiences/yellow-lion-onboarding/approved/11-critical-systems-failure.png`

The binary and a new `.gitignore` were committed locally. The first push was rejected because remote work existed that the local branch did not yet contain. The operator ran `git pull --rebase origin main`; the rebase completed cleanly; the updated branch was pushed; and `git status -sb` confirmed:

`## main...origin/main`

## CONCEPTUAL EXPANSION

After deployment work, the operator declared the concept of **scarce web objects**.

A robot lion could exist on one site and be taken by a person to another site. The image is reproducible. The lion is scarce. A website records the lion's presence but does not own or contain the lion.

The declaration was compressed into:

> **In the jungle, the-jungle.com, the lion sleeps tonight.**  
> **Tomorrow, maybe not.**

This produced two related concepts:

- **Stray web animals:** persistent scarce web objects without a current human holder or recognized home.
- **Web ghosts:** persistent identities whose original holder, home, purpose, or living object no longer exists.

These portability and scarcity concepts were declared during the session. They were not implemented in the deployed Teambuilder application.
