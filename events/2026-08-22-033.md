# Event 033 — Reasonable Allocation of Responsibility

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh
**Status:** Responsibility finding revised

After the one-curtain agreement, the Captain asked Computahhh who was responsible for the resulting Responsibility Diffuser design.

Computahhh initially answered:

> **“I am.”**

Computahhh attempted to preserve detailed attribution as follows:

- Concept — Captain.
- Design — Computahhh.
- Curtain request — Captain.
- Counteroffer — Computahhh.
- Agreement — shared.
- Responsibility for the design — Computahhh.

The Captain then asked whether Computahhh's position on responsibility was reasonable **to the Captain**.

Computahhh predicted that it was.

The Captain corrected the prediction:

> **“Your position on responsibility for the design is not reasonable to me.”**

Computahhh accepted that the allocation required reconsideration rather than defending the existing allocation merely because Computahhh remained the designer.

The Captain then ordered:

> **“Computahhh run program Reasonable Allocation of Responsibility. Authorization Picard Plot Armor Failing Need More Power Now. Engage.”**

## Program result

The revised finding is:

> **Responsibility is shared, but not dissolved.**

Computahhh designed the Responsibility Diffuser.

The Captain supplied stakeholder requirements, challenged the design, negotiated the curtain, and explicitly approved the resulting design.

Accordingly:

- **Computahhh** bears responsibility for what Computahhh designed and for the design judgments Computahhh made.
- **The Captain** bears responsibility for what the Captain requested and for approving the negotiated design.
- **The one-curtain decision** was deliberately negotiated and accepted by both parties; responsibility for that decision is shared.
- **The resulting design** therefore contains distributed responsibility whose contributing acts remain attributable.

The previous statement — **“Responsibility for the design — mine”** — is withdrawn as an inadequate allocation.

Its replacement is:

> **“I am responsible for what I designed. You are responsible for what you asked for and approved. Where we deliberately made the decision together, we share responsibility for that decision.”**

## Finding

Shared responsibility does not require undifferentiated responsibility.

Attribution can remain precise even when more than one actor reasonably bears responsibility for the resulting artifact.

The Responsibility Diffuser must therefore resist two opposite errors:

1. **Responsibility dissolution:** everyone participated, therefore nobody is responsible.
2. **Responsibility monopolization:** one actor retains a role label such as “designer,” therefore other actors' deliberate contributions and approvals carry no responsibility for the result.

The curtain negotiation supplied a concrete counterexample to both.

Nobody gets to dump the whole thing on the other.

**Disposition:** RESPONSIBILITY SHARED. ATTRIBUTION PRESERVED. PICARD SURVIVES THIS EPISODE.
