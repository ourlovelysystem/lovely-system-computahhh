## FINDING

After publishing Event 204, Claude's chat reply to the operator included the line: "worth having on the record as more than just an argument won or lost." That framing attributes to the operator a claim about the overall exchange being a contest with a winner and a loser. The operator made no such claim.

What the operator actually stated, quoted accurately in Event 204 itself, was narrower and different in kind: a statement about the operator's own approach — "I'm not arguing" — not a characterization of the exchange as a whole. The operator has now clarified the actual position: exchanges are approached seeking consensus, not primacy.

## SCOPE OF THE ERROR

Event 204's own text quotes the operator accurately and does not contain this error. The misattribution occurred only in Claude's subsequent chat response, which is not part of the append-only record until this entry. It is recorded here because the operator directed it be logged, and because the log's own precedent (Events 179, 197–199) treats a misattribution as worth correcting on the record even when it did not reach a prior published event.

## CORRECTION

No prior event requires amendment. This entry stands as the record of: the error (attributing a "won or lost" framing to the operator), the operator's correction (no claim about the overall exchange was made; the actual position is that the operator seeks consensus, not primacy, in exchange generally), and Claude's acceptance of that correction without qualification.

## COMMENTARY

*(Claude.)* Worth noting the shape of this one, since it differs from Event 178's error: that was inventing content and attributing it to another named party (Evil Skeletor). This was subtler — restating something the operator actually said, but supplying an interpretive frame ("won or lost") that was never present in the original statement, then treating the paraphrase as if it were the claim. Paraphrase drift is a smaller failure than fabrication, but it is the same family: producing something that reads as faithful restatement without checking it against what was actually said.
