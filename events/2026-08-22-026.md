# Event 026 — Design of the Responsibility Diffuser

**Date:** 2026-08-22
**System:** Our Lovely System
**Status:** Initial design proposed by Computahhh

The Captain instructed Computahhh to design the newly named **Responsibility Diffuser** and place that design in the event log.

## Purpose

The Responsibility Diffuser is a system for preventing one person's willingness to argue, escalate, decide, execute, or conceal from silently becoming total authority.

It does **not** make responsibility disappear.

It decomposes responsibility into bounded acts and assigns those acts to different roles, while preserving a durable record of who did what.

Its operating premise is:

> **Power may be distributed without laundering responsibility.**

## Core design

A consequential action is separated into distinct stages:

```text
PROPOSE
   ↓
QUESTION / OBJECT
   ↓
AUTHORIZE
   ↓
EXECUTE
   ↓
RECORD
   ↓
REVIEW
```

No stage automatically owns the next one.

A proposer can propose without possessing execution authority.

An objector can object without acquiring veto power merely by escalating.

An authorizer can approve without being able to conceal execution.

An executor can act without rewriting the authorization after the fact.

A recorder can preserve what happened without becoming the adjudicator of whether it was right.

A reviewer can inspect or challenge the result without retroactively pretending the original decision never occurred.

## Escalation rule

Argument is treated as **information production**, not adjudication.

Repeating an objection more loudly or more persistently does not increase its formal weight.

The system must therefore distinguish:

- strength of an objection;
- authority to decide;
- willingness to escalate.

These are not the same thing.

## Logical layers

The Responsibility Diffuser may place logical layers between the company nucleus and governors, trusted users, employees, or other participants.

Messages and actions may be transformed by authorized layers before they become consequences.

A person may originate an action without possessing unilateral control over what that action ultimately does.

Likewise, a governor may constrain an action without becoming sovereign over the company nucleus.

Trusted users or employees may influence governance without thereby acquiring gubernatorial authority.

## Advertised boundaries

Each layer should expose a MIMA-like boundary:

> **This is what I am. This is what I will not do.**

The relationship is reciprocal. One actor cannot force another across an advertised boundary merely because cooperation is valuable.

Technical controls may implement these bonds in different ways, including distributed authorization, access-controlled keys, voting mechanisms, constrained credentials, automatic publication of exceptional actions, or Self Destruct-style consequences.

The implementation is valid only if the control is faithful to the protocol rather than ceremonial.

## Emergency powers

Some circumstances cannot be safely reduced to ordinary machine process.

A vote or other declared governance mechanism may authorize emergency powers.

The exercise of those powers should create visible, durable records likely to surface to governors, trustees, operators, or other relevant observers.

Emergency authority may permit action outside ordinary process, but it should not silently erase the fact that ordinary process was bypassed.

## Failure mode to avoid

A bad Responsibility Diffuser becomes a **responsibility dissolver**:

> everyone participated, therefore nobody is responsible.

That is prohibited by design.

Diffusion means that responsibility is decomposed and attributable, not made deniable.

The system should be able to answer questions such as:

- Who proposed this?
- Who objected?
- Who authorized it?
- Who executed it?
- Under what authority?
- What exceptional powers were used?
- What record was created?
- Who later reviewed or challenged it?

## Initial invariant

> **No actor should gain additional decision weight merely by being more willing to escalate, and no actor should lose attribution merely because responsibility is shared.**
