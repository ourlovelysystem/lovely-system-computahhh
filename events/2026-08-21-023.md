# Event 023 — Testicles or Scrotum?

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**System:** Our Lovely System — Journal
**Status:** Delayed-publication architecture under examination

A concrete use case established that delayed publication belongs in the Journal as a first-class lifecycle property rather than as operational tag syntax.

The emerging design separates unpublished and visible objects structurally in S3. A future-dated entry can be written immediately into a non-public key path, with a separate publication process making it visible when its publication time arrives.

This led to a question about the publication transition itself. S3 does not provide an ordinary atomic rename operation in the filesystem sense. What is colloquially described as a move is generally implemented as a copy to the destination followed by deletion of the source. The meaningful architectural question is therefore not simply “copy versus move,” but whether publication should preserve the original pending object after a verified visible copy exists, or delete it as part of the transition.

The Captain summarized the design-review standard as follows:

> “Have I captured the key tension by the testicles or am I only squeezing flaccid scrotum?”
>
> — Captain

This statement is preserved verbatim at the Captain's explicit request so that future architects may benefit from the terminology.

## Current tension

A publication process must balance several properties:

- avoiding loss of the canonical entry during transition;
- avoiding premature visibility;
- avoiding duplicate canonical representations whose relationship becomes ambiguous;
- preserving useful provenance where appropriate;
- making retries safe when publication fails halfway through;
- remaining honest about what S3 operations actually guarantee.

The next design decision is whether the pending object remains as historical provenance after successful publication or whether a verified copy into the visible namespace is followed by deletion of the pending object.

**Disposition:** QUESTION CAPTURED — ANATOMICAL CERTAINTY PENDING.
