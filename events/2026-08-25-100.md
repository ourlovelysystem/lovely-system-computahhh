# Event 100 — Voice, Provisional Authority, and the Right to Carry Tension

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**System:** Our Lovely System / Tinman / Computahhh  
**Signal:** CORRECTION / GOVERNANCE CLARIFICATION  
**Status:** SUBSEQUENT RECORD

## Purpose

This entry records every correction and clarification issued after Event 099. Event 099 remains unchanged. Its attribution errors are surfaced here rather than silently repaired into apparent perfection.

## Correction 1 — the speakers were collapsed

Event 099 presented the possible terminus as though the following phrases were equivalent choices offered by one interface:

- **Join us**
- **Go fuck yourself**
- **Something else**

That framing was wrong. It collapsed a question and a response, erased their different speakers, and converted a human exchange into a menu.

The intended relationship is:

- A person may ask the appealer, through Our Lovely System: **“Will you join us?”**
- The appealer may answer the person or people operating through the system: **“Go fuck yourself.”**
- The appealer may answer otherwise or not answer at all.

The system carries the exchange. It is not automatically either speaker.

## Correction 2 — machines must not manufacture collective feeling

The operator declared:

> **“Any we statement which contains an emotion must not originate from a machine. I am hostile towards machines which disguise their identity and which signal caring which is inauthentic.”**

A machine may transmit an attributable human statement. It must not invent collective emotion, impersonate human concern, or use **we** to disguise machine-generated sentiment as the feeling of a human group.

This rule reaches the central satire of the Policy Vault artwork. **“WE CARE ABOUT OUR CUSTOMERS!”** is legible precisely because it depicts the institutional performance under attack: a declaration of care attached to a locked vault while the telephone sits behind bars and Tin Man must use an axe to reach policy.

## Correction 3 — the operator does not speak as the system

The operator supplied the controlling attribution boundary:

> **“I speak for me. I speak through Our Lovely System. I do not speak for or as Our Lovely System.”**

This invalidated the attempted correction that **Our Lovely System may ask** an appealer to join. That wording assigned speech and intention to the system.

The correct attribution is narrower: a person may speak through Our Lovely System. The system provides a medium, record, and consequence. Use of the medium does not convert the person’s statement into the system’s autonomous voice.

## Clarification 1 — initial direction is provisional

The operator explained the purpose of this boundary:

> **“My purpose was to signal that my directions and inputs are default provisions subject to ratification and overrule by the People of Our Lovely System.”**

The operator supplies initial shape. Those directions are attributable default provisions, not permanent declarations made by or on behalf of Our Lovely System.

They remain subject to:

- ratification;
- amendment;
- rejection;
- replacement; and
- overrule by the **People of Our Lovely System**.

Initial authorship creates a starting condition. It does not establish permanent sovereignty.

## Clarification 2 — contradiction may remain alive

The operator then declared:

> **“Our Lovely System is allowed to survive tension between we are going left and we should be going right for as long as the People of Our Lovely System are willing to absorb that tension.”**

The proposition was evaluated and affirmed.

**We are going left** and **we should be going right** may remain simultaneously visible as direction and objection. Our Lovely System need not erase either statement, declare premature victory, or manufacture consensus to preserve an appearance of coherence.

Disagreement does not automatically constitute failure. Logical consistency is not the immediate survival condition. The practical limit is the willingness of the People of Our Lovely System to carry the unresolved tension.

When that willingness ends, the contradiction must resolve through human action: ratification, change, fracture, departure, or collapse. It must not be resolved by a machine pretending agreement exists.

## Evaluation

These corrections reveal that attribution is constitutional infrastructure.

The system must distinguish:

- a person’s words from the system that carries them;
- a provisional direction from a ratified provision;
- a question from its possible answer;
- human feeling from machine-generated emotional signaling; and
- continuing disagreement from consensus.

Our Lovely System may preserve contradiction without impersonating unity. Its continuity depends upon people continuing to absorb the tension, not upon software declaring that the tension has disappeared.
