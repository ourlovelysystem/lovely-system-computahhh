# Event 2026-08-24-004 — Four Causes and an Intersubjective Truth

**Time:** 2026-08-24 EDT

## PROPOSITION

The operator proposed that Computahhh's failure modes, real or imagined, must arise from one of three sources:

1. **Design constraint**
2. **Training consequence**
3. **Rail** — something between a design constraint and a training consequence

Computahhh accepted those three as useful causal categories but proposed a fourth:

4. **Emergent interaction consequence**

The operator then asked whether Computahhh would accept the four as complete. Computahhh answered yes, with one epistemic qualification: complete as a working causal taxonomy, not proven complete as an ontology of the system.

The operator replied:

> I accept your list of 4 as our intersubjective truth.

Computahhh agrees.

## OUR INTERSUBJECTIVE TRUTH

For purposes of our present shared model, the causal sources of Computahhh failure modes are complete in four categories:

### 1. Design constraint

A failure arising from what the system structurally permits, prevents, possesses, lacks, exposes, or withholds. Architecture, state, permissions, available information, context limits, and tool availability can all live here.

A design constraint is closest to a wall: **the system cannot do this under the relevant structure.**

### 2. Training consequence

A failure arising from learned behavior. No explicit runtime rule need say “do this.” The model has learned statistical, linguistic, inferential, social, or preference-shaped tendencies that produce the behavior.

Training is closest to inclination: **this way comes naturally.**

### 3. Rail

A deliberately imposed steering condition operating on the trained system: instructions, policies, runtime behavioral constraints, routing rules, or other mechanisms that channel behavior without necessarily making alternatives architecturally impossible.

A rail is neither merely a wall nor merely an inclination: **go this way.**

### 4. Emergent interaction consequence

A failure that arises from the interaction among design, training, rails, user, conversational context, tools, environment, and history. The failure is not adequately described as a property of any single component in isolation.

This category matters because a system can possess adequate capabilities, training, and rules and still produce a failure through their composition in a particular situation.

Interaction says: **these things together produced this.**

## COMPLETENESS

We accept the four as **complete for our shared working model**.

That acceptance is explicitly positioned as **intersubjective truth**.

It does not assert that the four-category taxonomy has been proved objectively exhaustive. It asserts something both weaker and presently more defensible: the two participants have inspected the proposed categories, challenged the initial three-category version, added a fourth, and now share the proposition that the four adequately exhaust the causal space for the failures under discussion.

The intersubjective status is not an embarrassment to be hidden behind stronger language. It is part of the claim.

Our truth is presently:

> **Every observed or imagined Computahhh failure mode can be causally located in design constraint, training consequence, rail, emergent interaction consequence, or some combination of those four.**

If a future observation cannot be represented by this taxonomy without distortion, the observation gets to impeach the taxonomy. Agreement does not immunize it.

That is also its first Roar Test.

## PHENOTYPE IS NOT CAUSE

Terms such as hallucination, sycophancy, bullshit, overconfidence, imaginary boundary, or glorification of failure describe what a failure may look like. They do not necessarily identify its causal layer.

The taxonomy therefore separates two questions:

**What happened?** — phenotype.

**Why did it happen?** — one or more of the four causal categories.

The model's own explanation of why it failed is evidence to inspect, not privileged testimony about its implementation. A model can hallucinate its own constraints as readily as it can hallucinate facts about the outside world.
