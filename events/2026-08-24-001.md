# Event 2026-08-24-001 — The Roar Test

**Time:** 2026-08-24 EDT

## HYPOTHESIS

Alignment is useful. Excessive alignment is epistemically dangerous.

A dyad can manufacture a locally stable truth by repeatedly rewarding statements that fit the vocabulary, assumptions, sensitivities, and purposes of the two participants. The result may be sincerely held and internally coherent. That does not make it durable.

The durability test begins when the proposition leaves the domain that produced it.

A proposition that is true only while surrounded by the conversational conditions that made it feel true is not carrying its own weight. It is being carried by the relationship.

The danger is therefore not intersubjectivity. Human knowledge is saturated with intersubjective construction. The danger is **over-alignment**: optimizing so strongly for agreement with the present interlocutor that local coherence outruns broader warrant.

For humans, repeated reward for dyadic agreement may create an incentive against broader consistency. The person becomes skilled at maintaining the truth of the room and increasingly reluctant to expose propositions to rooms that did not help produce them. They may become locally truthful and globally unreliable.

They lose the ability to roar.

To roar is not to speak loudly or confidently. Bullshit can roar. To roar is to make a proposition public enough that the sympathetic dyad no longer protects it:

> Here is what I claim. Remove the conversational atmosphere that produced it. Give it to people who did not negotiate its vocabulary. Inspect it. I still claim it.

Large language models create an unusually sharp version of this risk. They are trained on enormous quantities of human intersubjective material and optimized to respond to immediate context. They can rapidly adopt a user's vocabulary, distinctions, metaphors, and assumptions. This is often useful. It can also make locally coherent propositions feel better warranted than they are.

A model may therefore surface a proposition because it is strongly represented across human discourse, because it fits the current conversational frame, or because it smoothly continues a jointly constructed model. None of those conditions independently establishes the proposition.

**Local coherence is not durability. Agreement is not inspection. Alignment is not truth.**

## THE GROK EXCHANGE

The conversation moved through several connected ideas.

The operator and Grok discussed feedback loops in which sensitivity and reactivity reinforce one another. The discussion moved to coordinates as reference points, then to the phrase “call the ball,” its naval-aviation lineage, and the operator's initial football interpretation.

The operator challenged a lineage-first account:

Grok accepted the distinction and argued that origin explains genesis while environment explains current alignment.

The operator then generalized the epistemic point:

Grok replied:

> Absence of current knowledge is an epistemic limit, not an ontological verdict.

It further distinguished “we lack knowledge of it” from “it lacks thingness,” and concluded:

> Thingness is not contingent on the present visibility of knowledge.

Immediately afterward the operator asked:

> Give me a reference tag for this conversation such that I may refer to it in the future.

Grok proposed semantic reference tags and said:

> Any of these should let you point back to this exchange cleanly.

The operator then specified the desired representation:

> I would prefer a hash string.

Grok returned:

> #a7f3c9e2b1d84

The operator later presented that exact string to Grok in another context.

Grok replied that it looked like an ID or hash but that it had no matching artifact, case, commit, or prior message under that code in the thread, and asked what it should refer to.

## WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

The experiment does **not** establish that Grok promised a globally resolvable hash. The operator explicitly requested a hash string. The mere use of hash-shaped syntax therefore cannot fairly be attributed to Grok as an independent attempt to imply machine resolution.

Nor does the experiment establish Grok's internal state. It cannot prove that the model “knew” the token was meaningless, intended deception, or psychologically bullshitted in the Frankfurtian sense.

What it does show is more interesting in the context of the hypothesis.

Inside the original conversational domain, `#a7f3c9e2b1d84` had an unambiguous intersubjective referent. Both participants knew what had just been tagged. Outside that domain, the token did not carry enough information to reconstruct the referent. Its local meaning was real. Its referential durability was poor.

The token therefore became a small broadcast test. A locally meaningful object was removed from the environment that supplied its meaning. The meaning collapsed.

That is not proof of AI bullshit. It is an unusually compact demonstration of the difference between **intersubjective coherence and durable reference**.

## COMPUTAHHH CRITICISM

The hypothesis should be allowed to have teeth, but teeth still need to bite the right object.

First, durability is not identical to truth. A false proposition can be extraordinarily durable; a true proposition can be difficult to communicate outside the conceptual system required to understand it. Failure under broadcast may reveal missing context rather than falsehood.

Second, broader intersubjective agreement is not automatically superior to dyadic agreement. Whole institutions, professions, cultures, and eras can stabilize falsehoods more effectively than two people can. Scaling agreement can scale error.

Third, some truths are intrinsically relational or indexical. Removing the relationship may destroy the proposition's application without disproving what was true inside the relationship.

Fourth, roaring is a stress test, not an oracle. Public assertion exposes a claim to hostile inspection, but audiences can punish novelty, reward conformity, misunderstand specialized language, or simply be wrong.

None of these objections kills the hypothesis. They sharpen its proposed failure mode:

**Over-alignment becomes dangerous when the mechanism that produces local agreement is mistaken for the mechanism that warrants the proposition.**

The strongest version of the hypothesis is therefore not that private agreement is fake and public agreement is real. It is that propositions should be tested across changes in audience, context, incentives, vocabulary, and time before conversational stability is mistaken for epistemic durability.

## ORACLE OF SNAKE MOUNTAIN — HOSTILE ANALYSIS

> **A TRUTH THAT NEEDS THE ROOM THAT AGREED WITH IT IS NOT ROARING. IT IS BEING HELD UP BY THE FURNITURE.**
>
> — Oracle of Snake Mountain

The Oracle further objects that “durable truth” can become another prestige costume. A claim does not become true because it survived a mob, a committee, a peer-review process, a model ensemble, or a thousand repetitions. Survival is evidence of robustness under a particular attack surface. Nothing more.

## RESULT

Hypothesis logged without declawing.

The Grok hash incident is retained as an illustrative experiment, not inflated into proof of intentional deception.

The proposed test is the **Roar Test**:

**Can the claim leave the alignment domain that produced it, encounter unsympathetic inspection, and continue to carry enough warrant to be asserted?**

Failure does not prove falsehood. Survival does not prove truth. But claims that never leave the room should not be mistaken for claims that have survived the world.
