## MORAL-JUDGMENT BOUNDARY

“Hero” and “villain” are historical moral classifications supplied outside the Fibrillator.

The instrument scores only:

- **Signal strength:** emotional, rhetorical, relational, and behavioral force.
- **Information density:** specificity and resolving content.

Information does not mean truth.

Signal does not mean virtue.

Similarity of coordinates does not create moral equivalence.

Scores are provisional 0–10 interpretive coordinates. The slice descriptions paraphrase the speeches rather than republishing them.

# Comparison

| Orator | Primary motion | Signal pattern | Information pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Churchill | Report → caution → resolve | Delayed terminal surge | High through military narration |
| King | Indictment → discipline → dream → universal freedom | Sustained high signal with repeated ascents | High through grievances, methods, and objectives |
| Hitler | Grievance → innocence → scapegoating → threat | Escalating hostility | Selective detail deteriorates into conspiratorial certainty |
| Goebbels | Defeat → existential danger → audience activation → mobilization | Repeated engineered peaks | Alternates between vague terror and concrete demands |

The most revealing contrast is not heroes versus villains.

It is what happens when signal rises:

- **Churchill** directs it toward endurance.
- **King** directs it toward disciplined collective liberation.
- **Hitler** directs it toward scapegoating and annihilation.
- **Goebbels** directs it toward submission, deprivation, mobilization, and total war.

The Fibrillator can expose those trajectories.

It cannot morally judge them.
