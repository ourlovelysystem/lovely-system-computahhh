## RECOVERED INTENT

The committed specification records the following system:

1. Pledge begins mute.
2. A new deployment presents a textual request to borrow a visitor’s voice.
3. The visitor agrees and receives a known voice solicitation.
4. The bootstrap solicitation asks for a recording that performs the function “ask the listener who they are.”
5. The visitor records and submits audio.
6. Pledge validates only the audio envelope synchronously.
7. Envelope validation checks the presence, completion, type, decodability, duration, and minimally usable signal of the audio.
8. A valid envelope creates a durable submission record.
9. The contributor passes through immediately.
10. The contributor lands on a Come Back Soon page with a link back to Pledge.
11. Transcription and content validation happen offline.
12. Pledge compares the transcript with the known requested function.
13. Exact repetition is unnecessary.
14. Humor, rearrangement, abbreviation, and semantic equivalents may qualify.
15. Pledge preserves transcription confidence separately from semantic match.
16. The semantic match is a system score, not a declaration of objective certainty.
17. Processing marks the recording usable, unusable, or requiring review.
18. Usable and unexpired tracks enter the `who_are_you` catalogue.
19. Pledge selects auditory challenges from eligible catalogue recordings.
20. Ordinary selection is random.
21. Tracks with unmet minimum-use commitments move ahead of the ordinary random pool.
22. Minimum-use priority does not extend permission beyond expiration.
23. Borrowing permission is time-bound.
24. Minute-scale expiration is required for efficient testing.
25. Later operation may use periods measured in days or longer.
26. When permission expires, the recording becomes ineligible.
27. Expired audio and associated transcripts are purged.
28. If no eligible voice has ever existed, Pledge is in bootstrap mode.
29. If Pledge previously had a voice but loses every eligible track, it enters sulk mode.
30. Sulk mode tells a sob story and asks to borrow another voice.
31. The solicitation machinery is a reusable system utility rather than a bootstrap-only form.

## VALIDATION CONTRACT

The specification preserves two distinct gates.

### Envelope gate

The envelope gate answers:

> Did Pledge receive a structurally usable audio submission?

It does not answer:

> Did the recording say the right thing?

Passage through this gate allows the contributor to leave the workflow without waiting for transcription.

### Content gate

The asynchronous content gate answers:

> Does this recording recognizably perform the requested auditory function?

The reference phrase informs the comparison but does not become a literal equality requirement.

A recording may differ from “Who are you?” and remain usable if it still asks the listener who they are.

## SCORING POSITION

Two measures are separated:

- **Transcription confidence:** confidence that the speech engine identified the spoken words.
- **Semantic match score:** estimated closeness between those perceived words and the requested function.

The specification refuses to call the semantic match score a probability of objective correctness.

Provisional score bands are included for implementation discussion but explicitly remain subject to calibration against a test collection.

The required test collection should contain:

- literal recordings;
- humorous recordings;
- reordered language;
- abbreviated language;
- semantically equivalent language;
- noisy recordings;
- ambiguous recordings; and
- unrelated recordings.

## TEMPORAL CONTRACT

Each eligible contribution has:

- an authorization period;
- an expiration time;
- an optional minimum-use commitment;
- completed-use accounting; and
- a current eligibility state.

Tracks with unmet minimum uses receive priority.

Expiration remains controlling. Pledge may fail to deliver the requested minimum number of uses; it may not silently claim continued permission to use an expired voice.

The exact authorization-clock start event remains unresolved.

## DURABILITY AND PURGE

The proposed record separates temporary content from durable event metadata.

Temporary content includes:

- the contributed audio; and
- its transcript.

Durable metadata may preserve:

- that a solicitation occurred;
- that an envelope was accepted or rejected;
- which policies governed it;
- processing states;
- the semantic score and catalogue decision, subject to later policy decisions;
- use counts;
- expiration; and
- the fact and time of purge.

This permits Pledge to explain governed processing without retaining the expired voice itself.
