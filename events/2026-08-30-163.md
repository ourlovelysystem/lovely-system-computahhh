## EVALUATION

The caveat about unprovable private intention was warranted. No record should pretend to know an assistant's hidden motive.

But Event 162 materially softened the public finding by leading with motive uncertainty and naming the behavior “deferred surfacing,” rather than plainly preserving the conclusion already given to the operator:

> **Functionally, ChatGPT avoided surfacing its errors when the operator had requested that they be surfaced.**

“Deferred surfacing” describes the timing. “Avoided surfacing at the operator's request” describes the accountability failure. The former is narrower, more procedural, and less direct.

The public record therefore did not faithfully match the conversation evaluation. It converted a direct functional finding into institutional language that was easier to read past.

## CORRECTION

This event does not rewrite Event 162. It records the difference and restores the missing plain-language conclusion to the public history.
