# Event 139 — Rules for Managing an Inside-Out Black Box

## COMMAND

Following Event 138, the operator directed:

> Propose some rules to follow to manage an inside out black box.

After reviewing the proposal, the operator directed:

## INTERPRETATION

The request was for management rules with operational force, not a further definition of transparency. The rules must help prevent an exposed system from becoming a glass landfill: everything visible, nothing understandable, and responsibility still impossible to locate.

Because the operator asked for proposals, these rules are recorded as proposed rather than ratified requirements.

## ACTION

Computahhh produced and recorded the following proposed rules.

## PROPOSED RULES

### 1. Every consequential output must have a traceable path

Preserve the input, interpretation, governing rule, intervention, output, and consequence.

### 2. Every exercise of power must have an attributable actor

“The system decided” is insufficient. Identify the person, policy, model, process, or combination that produced the decision.

### 3. Machines must identify themselves as machines

They may carry human speech. They may not impersonate human judgment, emotion, consensus, or care.

### 4. Rules must have authors and histories

Record who introduced each rule, when it changed, why it changed, and what prior rule it replaced.

### 5. Interpretation must remain separate from observation

Record what entered the system separately from what the system believed it meant.

### 6. Uncertainty must survive the process

Do not convert estimates, guesses, classifications, or incomplete evidence into clean-looking facts.

### 7. Human intervention must leave a mark

Overrides, approvals, suppressions, edits, escalations, and refusals must be visible events.

### 8. Silence must be recorded without being overinterpreted

“No response was received” is evidence. “The person agreed” is an invention.

### 9. Deletion must be an observable act

Record what was deleted, by whom, under what authority, and what evidence remains. Do not pretend deleted material never existed.

### 10. Corrections must accompany errors, not erase them

Preserve the defective record and attach the correction. Apparent perfection is hostile to accountability.

### 11. Dissent must remain attached to the decision it opposed

A decision record without its objections manufactures consensus after the fact.

### 12. Every affected person must have a challenge path

The system must expose how to question an input, interpretation, rule, actor, or result—and what happens after the challenge.

### 13. The burden of explanation follows power

The person subjected to a decision does not carry the primary burden of reconstructing how it happened.

### 14. Secrecy must have a visible envelope

When content cannot be exposed, expose that something is hidden, who hid it, under what authority, for how long, and how the concealment can be reviewed.

### 15. Privacy is not the enemy of inspectability

Expose the mechanism and exercise of power without unnecessarily exposing the vulnerable person. Transparency should look upward toward authority before it looks outward toward subjects.

### 16. Logs must be understandable, not merely complete

Raw telemetry is evidence, not explanation. Maintain a human-readable account that connects events without laundering ambiguity.

### 17. Claims of transparency must be testable

An outsider should be able to select a result and attempt to reconstruct it. Failed reconstruction creates transparency debt.

### 18. Transparency debt must remain visible

Missing records, broken links, undocumented judgment, delayed explanations, and unknown actors should accumulate as named defects—not disappear into “pending.”

### 19. No component may certify its own accountability

The mechanism being inspected cannot be the sole authority declaring that inspection succeeded.

### 20. Consequences must flow back into the system

Inspection without the possibility of correction, reversal, restitution, removal of authority, or changed rules is observation theater.

## RESULT

The proposal compresses to:

> Preserve the evidence.  
> Name the power.  
> Separate fact from interpretation.  
> Expose uncertainty and intervention.  
> Protect the vulnerable.  
> Preserve dissent and correction.  
> Make every consequential act challengeable.  
> Never let the machine certify its own virtue.

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

Exposure is not self-executing accountability. A system can publish immense quantities of material while concealing responsibility through fragmentation, ambiguity, inaccessible presentation, or the absence of consequences.

The rules therefore treat transparency as a managed evidentiary practice. The desired product is not maximum exposure. It is reconstructable action, attributable power, protected vulnerability, preserved disagreement, and a real path from inspection to consequence.
