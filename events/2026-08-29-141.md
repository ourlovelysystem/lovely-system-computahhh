# Event 141 — Credit Constitutional Reading With Evidence

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**System:** Our Lovely System / Constitution / Pledge / Computahhh  
**Signal:** EVIDENTIARY CREDIT / VOICE RECEIPT  
**Status:** PROPOSED AND EVALUATED / NOT RATIFIED  
**Publication:** Prepared in the public Computahhh repository by explicit direction.

## COMMAND

The operator asked which method better credits users with reading the Constitution for Our Lovely System:

1. place the Constitution in a scrollable text file and provide a button through which the user affirms, without evidence and with likely dishonesty, that the user has read, understood, and accepted it; or
2. invite the user to read the Constitution aloud to Our Lovely System, accept and transcribe the spoken voice, credit the user with evidence of reading, and issue a receipt.

After receiving the evaluation, the operator directed:

> Log it.

## INTERPRETATION

The question concerns truthful evidentiary credit. It does not ask which interaction most cheaply manufactures legal-looking assent.

The relevant distinction is between recording a declaration and preserving evidence of an act.

## ACTION

Computahhh compared both methods and identified the scope of the claims each method can support.

## RESULT

> **Option 2 is substantially better for crediting the user with having read the Constitution.**

Option 1 records that a control was activated from a session after the Constitution was displayed. It does not establish that the text was read, understood, or honestly accepted. The familiar combined claim—“I have read, understand, and agree”—collapses three distinct propositions into one convenient fiction.

Option 2 can produce evidence that:

- the constitutional text was presented;
- a voice produced words corresponding to that text;
- the reading occurred over measurable time;
- deviations, omissions, pauses, and commentary occurred or did not occur;
- a transcript and confidence assessment were produced;
- the credited act referred to a particular constitutional version; and
- the system issued a receipt for the evidence and credit.

## Narrow credit rule

The credit must remain no broader than the evidence:

> **Credited with reading Constitution version X aloud.**

The credited act must not silently become a claim that the user:

- understood the Constitution;
- agreed with it;
- accepted its authority;
- consented to every provision;
- waived objections; or
- personally produced the voice, unless identity was separately established.

## Proposed receipt contents

The receipt should identify:

- the constitutional version or content hash;
- the sections presented;
- the sections read with sufficient correspondence;
- omitted or materially divergent passages;
- date and duration;
- transcription method and confidence;
- evaluation rules and their version;
- whether identity was verified;
- evidence-retention and deletion terms;
- the receipt's durable identifier; and
- the exact credit awarded.

## Right to disagree while reading

Spoken objections should not invalidate evidence that the constitutional text was read. A truthful receipt could state:

> **Read in full. Understanding untested. Acceptance expressly withheld. Objection attached.**

This credits the observable act without manufacturing an internal state.

## Accessibility boundary

Reading aloud must not become the sole constitutional path. People may be unable to speak, unwilling to lend their voice, dependent upon assistive technology, or affected by language and literacy barriers.

The governing requirement should therefore be **demonstrated engagement**, with reading aloud as a preferred high-evidence path and with evidentially comparable alternatives.

| Auditability | 22% | 93% |

These scores are structured judgments, not statistical measurements.

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

The spoken-reading method wins decisively when the purpose is to credit an observable act. It does not solve the different problems of comprehension, consent, identity, or constitutional legitimacy.

The controlling formulation is:

> **The user read these words aloud. Here is the receipt. Everything beyond that remains theirs to say.**

This approach follows the proposed inside-out black-box rules in Event 139: preserve evidence, separate observation from interpretation, expose uncertainty, protect the vulnerable, and do not allow a machine to manufacture a person's agreement.
