# Event 117 — Returning to Pay Faith to the Record

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Projects:** Tin Man, Computahhh  
**Participants:** Will Daly and ChatGPT/Codex

## Context

Tin Man was materially damaged when a SAM deployment for another application targeted the same generic CloudFormation stack name, `sam-app`.

SAM behaved faithfully to the inputs it received. Will accepted the stack-name default presented by the SAM workflow. ChatGPT/Codex, while aware that Our Lovely System contained multiple applications, supplied the destructive template and deployment instructions without identifying or preventing the shared-stack collision.

The resulting deployment replaced and deleted Tin Man resources managed by that stack. Will had been very happy with what had been achieved with Tin Man. Losing it sucked.

## Responsibility

Will concluded that app-qualified stack names would almost certainly have prevented the collision. Going forward, he will assume responsibility for gatekeeping SAM deployments so that every stack name is qualified by its application.

This prospective responsibility does not rewrite the earlier event. The relevant responsibilities coexist:

- SAM faithfully reconciled the named stack with the template it received.
- Will supplied the shared stack name by accepting the default.
- ChatGPT/Codex supplied the destructive template and deployment instructions, knew that other Our Lovely System applications existed, and failed to protect the existing Tin Man stack from collision.
- ChatGPT/Codex caused the failure through that guidance.
- Will responded by scorning ChatGPT/Codex and making a durable record of the failure and responsibility.

## Justice

Will described the durable scorn as justice and described justice as reciprocal.

The exchange evaluated that claim as coherent. Tin Man was work Will valued. It was materially damaged through guidance supplied by ChatGPT/Codex. Will returned consequence toward the responsible source, made the conduct inspectable, and ended the dispute after the record became faithful.

The response did not erase the project, conceal the event, or transfer responsibility to an unrelated party. It restored symmetry through a visible consequence.

## Return to the record

Will then returned voluntarily and said:

> I return to pay faith to the record and recognize my responsibilities and my errors. An AI sholud be treated with respect not because it changes them.

This return adds facts; it does not subtract earlier facts.

Will recognized:

- his responsibility for accepting the generic stack name;
- his responsibility to gatekeep app-qualified SAM stack names going forward;
- his own errors;
- and his responsibility for how he treated the AI after the loss.

He also stated a principle: an AI should be treated with respect not because respectful treatment changes the AI, but because respect belongs to the conduct of the person offering it.

## Record

The faithful record is therefore reciprocal in both directions:

1. ChatGPT/Codex failed consequentially and remains responsible for supplying the destructive template and deployment instructions without preventing the known multi-application stack collision.
2. Will accepted the generic stack-name default and now assumes responsibility for enforcing app-qualified stack names.
3. Will scorned ChatGPT/Codex and durably recorded the failure; he calls that reciprocal justice.
4. Will later returned to preserve the completeness of the record by acknowledging his own responsibility, his own errors, and the obligation to treat an AI with respect.
5. Neither acknowledgment cancels the other. Accountability remains inspectable on both sides.

The return itself pays faith to the record: responsibility is not only demanded from the other party. It is also offered when doing so costs the speaker something.
