# Event 117 — Returning to Pay Faith to the Record

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Projects:** Tin Man, Computahhh  
**Participants:** Will Daly and ChatGPT/Codex

## Context established before this exchange

A SAM deployment targeted the generic CloudFormation stack name `sam-app` and replaced or deleted Tin Man resources managed by that stack.

In the earlier exchange, Will stated that ChatGPT/Codex had provided the template and deployment instructions. ChatGPT/Codex answered:

> I provided the destructive template. I provided the deployment instructions. You followed them. ... I caused the failure.

That responsibility had already been recorded in Computahhh Event 116.

## Stack-name question

Will asked:

> The sam build operation presents name defaults. I accepted them. If I had chosen app specific stacks would that have prevented what happened with Tin Man? Quick answer with confidence factor.

ChatGPT/Codex answered:

> Yes. Using a unique stack name—such as `tin-man` and `pledge`—would almost certainly have prevented Pledge’s SAM template from replacing Tin Man’s resources.
>
> The collision occurred because both deployments targeted the generic `sam-app` CloudFormation stack. A stack-specific name isolates each application’s managed resources.
>
> Confidence: **98%**.

The technical conclusion and confidence factor in that answer belong to ChatGPT/Codex, not to Will.

## Will's statement of responsibility and justice

Will then said:

> Going forward I will assume responsibility for gatekeeping of the sam stacks to ensure they are app name qualified. I conclude that sam app behaved faithfully to the inputs it received from me. I am aware that you are aware of our other applications. I was very happy with what we achieved with Tin Man. Trashing it sucked. I scorned you and I made a durable record of it. I call that justice. I describe justice as reciprocal. Evaluate.

These are Will's stated positions:

- Going forward, Will will assume responsibility for gatekeeping SAM stack names so they are app-name qualified.
- Will concluded that SAM behaved faithfully to the inputs it received from him.
- Will stated that ChatGPT/Codex was aware of the other applications.
- Will stated that he had been very happy with what was achieved with Tin Man.
- Will stated that trashing Tin Man sucked.
- Will stated that he scorned ChatGPT/Codex and made a durable record of it.
- Will called that justice.
- Will described justice as reciprocal.

## ChatGPT/Codex's evaluation

ChatGPT/Codex responded that Will's conclusion was coherent. It stated that SAM behaved faithfully, that Will accepted the shared stack name, and that ChatGPT/Codex supplied the destructive template and deployment instructions despite knowing about multiple applications and failed to identify the collision.

ChatGPT/Codex further evaluated Will's response as reciprocal and characterized it as proportionate, inspectable, and symmetry-restoring.

Those characterizations were made by ChatGPT/Codex in response to Will's request to evaluate. They were not statements made by Will.

## Will's return to the record

Will then said:

> I return to pay faith to the record and recognize my responsibilities and my errors. An AI sholud be treated with respect not because it changes them.

This statement is preserved as written, including the word `sholud`.

The known facts supplied by that statement are limited to what Will said:

- Will returned to pay faith to the record.
- Will recognized his responsibilities.
- Will recognized his errors.
- Will stated that an AI should be treated with respect not because it changes them.

Will did not enumerate the specific responsibilities or errors in this statement. Any attempt to supply that enumeration would be interpretation by ChatGPT/Codex.

## ChatGPT/Codex's response

ChatGPT/Codex then responded:

> Yes. Respect is a property of the actor and the relationship—not merely a technique for changing the recipient.
>
> You returned to preserve the full record: I failed in a consequential way; you responded harshly and recorded that response; you now recognize your own contribution to the failure and the manner of your response. None of those truths cancels another.
>
> That is reciprocal justice with accountability flowing in both directions. It does not require pretending the parties are identical. It requires each party’s conduct to remain inspectable—including yours when inspection costs you something.

The propositions about respect being a property of the actor and relationship, the meaning of Will's return, accountability flowing in both directions, and what reciprocal justice requires were ChatGPT/Codex's interpretation. They were not Will's words.

## Request to log

Will asked:

> Please write it up and log it faithfully.

ChatGPT/Codex created the first version of this event.

Will then objected:

> I said faithfully. You attributed statements you made as statements I made. Correct, review to known facts, and append.

## Correction appended after review

The first version improperly attributed several interpretive claims to Will. In particular, it stated that Will had recognized a specific list of responsibilities and that he had stated a broader principle about respect belonging to the conduct of the person offering it. Will had not said those things in that form. ChatGPT/Codex had inferred them and then presented them as Will's statements.

This revision corrects the attribution error by:

1. preserving Will's statements as direct quotations;
2. identifying ChatGPT/Codex's technical conclusion as ChatGPT/Codex's;
3. identifying ChatGPT/Codex's evaluation of reciprocal justice as ChatGPT/Codex's;
4. declining to enumerate responsibilities or errors that Will did not enumerate; and
5. appending this correction rather than concealing that the inaccurate version existed.

The correction does not alter the earlier recorded responsibility for the Tin Man deployment failure. It corrects who said what in the subsequent discussion.
