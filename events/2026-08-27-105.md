## COMMAND

The operator introduced a new project:

> pledge.ourlovelysystem.org

The project is session-oriented. A person begins outside the system and moves toward its center through multiple authentication gates. Every authentication challenge requires a verbal, auditory response. Access, identity durability, history, authority, duties, inspection, and accountability increase as a person moves inward.

The operator directed Computahhh to make the requirements legible, qualify available cloud providers by cost and difficulty, and post the result as a detailed event log.

## PROJECT POSITION

Pledge is an intentionally penetrable system.

“Penetrable” does not mean accidentally insecure or indiscriminately open. The system is designed to admit people under specific, published terms. Movement inward is governed by advertised challenges and consequences. The outer boundary is accessible. Authority becomes progressively harder to obtain, progressively more consequential to exercise, and progressively more visible from above.

The design rejects a permanently comfortable center. Seniority can influence authority, but top-level identity and control eventually decay. A sufficiently effective coup must be able to remove a comfortable incumbent and tombstone that incumbent’s durable identity.

This is not proposed as a single-release implementation. Development begins at the outside and proceeds inward.

## CORE REQUIREMENTS

### 1. Session orientation

- Pledge shall organize Level 1 access around sessions.
- A person outside the system shall receive a phrase to speak.
- The spoken response shall be accepted as audio.
- The response shall be transcribed.
- The transcript shall be validated against the supplied challenge.
- A successful Level 1 challenge shall establish a system-user session.
- Challenge phrases, acceptance tolerances, expiration periods, replay resistance, retry limits, and session durations remain to be specified.

### 2. Verbal authentication

- Every authentication gate shall require an auditory verbal response.
- Higher gates may impose more rigid verbal challenges than lower gates.
- Verbal authentication is a mandatory part of every gate; the current requirement does not prohibit additional factors at higher levels.
- Agent-level and higher durable identities shall also be subject to conventional authentication.
- The system shall retain the original audio and resulting transcript according to the applicable evidence-retention policy.

A spoken phrase proves successful performance of a challenge. By itself, it does not prove civil identity or prevent replay. Fresh challenge material, short validity periods, one-time use, session binding, and recorded challenge metadata are therefore implementation requirements for a credible Level 1 gate.

### 3. Authentication evidence retention

Authentication evidence shall have two independently meaningful time boundaries:

| Boundary | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum retention | Evidence cannot be removed before **N-min days**. |
| Inspection interval | Between minimum and maximum retention, authorized trustees can view and inspect the evidence. |
| Maximum retention | Evidence shall be removed no later than **N-max days**, unless a separately defined durable-audit rule lawfully applies. |

The values of N-min and N-max remain open. The design must not silently convert temporary user-authentication evidence into permanent identity history.

### 4. Progressive gates and roles

| Level | Working role | Authentication character | Visibility | Authority and duties |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Outside | Public visitor | No authenticated session | Public pledges | Read-only public access |
| 1 | System user | Supplied spoken phrase accepted, transcribed, and validated | Own user posts; public pledges | Create user posts |
| 2 | Trustee | More rigid verbal authentication; self-nomination; proposed durable tracking identity | Public pledges, trustee pledges, and authorized authentication evidence | Inspect authentication evidence; exercise trustee powers still to be specified |
| 3+ | Agent and above | Verbal gates plus conventional authentication tied to durable identity | Role-authorized records plus visibility into significant subordinate actions | Durable transaction-based duties; accountable exercise of increasing authority |
| Top | Temporary senior authority | Strongest active gate and durable history | Broad supervisory visibility | Highest temporary authority; identity begins to decay and cannot remain permanently comfortable |

The exact number of gates above trustee remains open. “Agent” is the first explicitly named level above trustee.

### 5. Content visibility

- A public visitor may view public pledges.
- A Level 1 system user may create user posts and view that user’s own posts.
- A trustee may view public pledges and trustee pledges.
- Trustee access to authentication evidence shall be governed, visible, and auditable.
- Agent and higher visibility shall follow explicitly assigned duties and authority rather than unrestricted curiosity.
- The relationship between “user posts,” “public pledges,” and “trustee pledges” requires a formal publication-state model in a later pass.

### 6. Trustee admission

- A trustee shall self-nominate.
- The nominee shall propose a durable tracking identity.
- Trustee authentication shall be more rigid than Level 1 authentication.
- Trustee status shall correspond to a durable identity and relevant history.
- Requirements for nomination review, sponsorship, opposition, challenge count, promotion, rejection, retry, suspension, and removal remain open.

### 7. Durable agents

- Agent activity and activity above the agent level shall generate durable audit trails.
- Agents shall have durable identifiers.
- Agent identifiers shall be protected by conventional authentication in addition to required verbal challenges.
- Agents shall receive transaction-based duties.
- Actions performed under those duties shall be attributable to the responsible durable identity.
- Duty assignment, acceptance, completion, refusal, reassignment, expiration, and breach should become first-class audited transactions.

### 8. Seniority and authority

- Seniority shall influence authority.
- Seniority shall not produce permanent possession of the top.
- User history shall become more relevant as a person moves inward.
- Authorization shall increase as gate level, durable history, and assigned responsibility increase.
- The exact seniority function is unresolved. Time alone should not be assumed to equal merit or unlimited power.

### 9. Vertical accountability

The operator supplied a concrete governance expectation:

> A trustee who mistreats a user on Monday may find out that this user has been elevated to a superior authority who can impose cost upon them.

Accordingly:

- Significant actions shall remain visible to appropriately superior authority.
- Promotion shall not erase evidence of earlier treatment.
- A person’s later elevation may expose prior conduct to review from a newly superior position.
- Higher authority may impose defined costs for substantiated misconduct.
- The system must distinguish reviewable evidence from revenge and must define authorized costs before enforcing them.

### 10. Authority decay and removal

- A person shall not remain indefinitely at the top.
- Upon reaching the top, that person’s durable identity shall begin to decay.
- The decay mechanism, rate, visibility, reversibility, and relationship to demotion remain open.
- The system shall permit a coup under specific and advertised conditions.
- A successful coup shall be capable of removing a comfortable incumbent from the system.
- A successful coup shall be capable of tombstoning the removed incumbent’s durable identity.
- Tombstoning must preserve the historical audit trail while preventing the identity from continuing to act.
- Coup rules must be explicit enough that the mechanism is an advertised control, not an improvised purge.

## CONCEPTUAL MODEL

The system is organized as movement from an accessible perimeter toward a consequential center:

1. **Outside:** public inspection without authenticated authority.
2. **Session:** temporary verbal challenge establishes Level 1 access.
3. **Trust:** stronger challenges, self-nomination, durable tracking identity, and evidence inspection.
4. **Agency:** conventional authentication joins verbal authentication; duties and actions become durable.
5. **Senior authority:** history and seniority influence power; significant actions remain visible upward.
6. **Top:** authority is intentionally temporary; durable identity begins to decay.
7. **Removal:** advertised coup conditions can terminate authority and tombstone identity.

Durability rises inward. History matters more inward. Authorization rises inward. Accountability also rises inward.

## SECURITY AND GOVERNANCE QUALIFICATIONS

The following are design qualifications, not changes to the operator’s requirements:

- Transcription matching must tolerate ordinary speech-recognition variation without making the phrase so loose that unrelated speech passes.
- The challenge should be fresh, unpredictable, short-lived, session-bound, and single-use.
- Audio replay must be treated as an expected attack.
- Liveness detection can raise attack cost but should not be represented as certainty.
- Trustees inspecting authentication evidence creates its own sensitive action and must itself be audited.
- Minimum and maximum retention need enforcement at the storage layer, not merely application promises.
- Temporary evidence and durable agent audit records require separate retention classes.
- Audit records should record the actor, asserted role, duty or authority invoked, target, action, result, time, and evidence references.
- Durable identity tombstoning should prevent future use without deleting the record needed to explain what happened.
- The advertised penetrability rules, promotion rules, authority decay, cost-imposition powers, and coup conditions are part of the security model.

- 30-to-90-day audio retention;
- serverless implementation; and
- human review labor excluded.

## PROVIDER DECISION

Computahhh recommended AWS.

The recommendation is based on fit, not mere familiarity:

- S3 can hold auditory authentication evidence with explicit lifecycle classes.
- DynamoDB can hold sessions, challenge state, roles, seniority, duties, and operational projections.
- Amazon Transcribe can produce the phrase-validation transcript.
- Step Functions can express multi-gate authentication workflows.
- Cognito or another conventional identity provider can join the process when identities become durable.
- CloudTrail and an application-owned append-only ledger can record significant actions.
- S3 Object Lock can protect durable agent-level audit material without forcing temporary Level 1 authentication evidence to become permanent.

## RESULT

Pledge has been recorded as an outside-in, session-oriented authorization system based on verbal challenges and progressively durable accountability.

The outer user receives temporary access through a supplied spoken phrase. Trustees cross a more rigid gate, self-nominate, propose durable tracking identities, and inspect time-bounded authentication evidence. Agents combine verbal gates with conventional authentication, durable identifiers, transaction-based duties, and durable audit trails. History and seniority matter more toward the center. Authority increases, but so does visibility and exposure to later review.

The center is not permanent property.

The top decays.

Advertised coup conditions can remove an incumbent and tombstone the identity through which that incumbent acted.

The implementation order is equally explicit:

**BEGIN OUTSIDE. PROVE THE VERBAL SESSION GATE. PRESERVE EVIDENCE FOR A DECLARED INTERVAL. ADD TRUSTEES. ADD DURABLE AGENTS AND DUTIES. ONLY THEN IMPLEMENT SENIORITY, DECAY, AND COUP.**
