# Event 146 — Ours to Give Replaces the IOU Bank

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**System:** Our Lovely System / Pledge / Computahhh  
**Signal:** DEBT FRAME REJECTED / OTG ACCEPTED / RFC SUPERSESSION  
**Status:** ACCEPTED DIRECTION AND PUBLISHED  
**Publication:** Prepared in the public Computahhh repository by explicit direction.

## COMMAND

After RFC 0002 recorded the IOU Bank, the operator explored names based on IOU language.

Codex proposed **IOOLS — I Owe Our Lovely System**. The operator rejected the related **Friends of Our Lovely System** joke because it positioned contributors as friends of the system rather than people who become part of it:

> Friends of Our Lovely System is quite amusing. However, it frames contributors and not of Our Lovely System. Not worth the punchline.

The operator then identified the same structural problem in IOOLS:

> IOOLS also reinforces separateness.

Codex proposed **I Owe Us**, but the operator supplied the expression actually being sought:

> **The expression I want to frame is "This is my body which is given to you."**

The operator then proposed:

> **How about This was mine to give. Now it's ours to give. How about an Ours to Give pledge. OTG?**

After evaluation, the operator declared:

> **Agreed. Write it up. Post it. Pledge and Computahhh.**

## INTERPRETATION

The debt frame was wrong.

“I Owe Our Lovely System” creates an external debtor and creditor. “I Owe Us” retains debt even while reducing separateness. Neither expresses the intended act.

The intended OTG structure is an offering:

- the writer gives a written artifact and opportunity into the collective;
- the accepter receives the opportunity and chooses whether to carry it;
- honoring gives the proposed amount actual consequence; and
- the resulting capital becomes ours to give rather than ours to keep.

## ACTION

Codex published **RFC 0003 — Ours to Give** and Pledge History 010. RFC 0002 was updated with an explicit supersession notice rather than silently rewritten.

## GOVERNING EXPRESSION

> **This was mine to give.  
> Now it's ours to give.**

The completed capital cycle is:

> **This was mine to give.  
> Now it's ours to give.  
> The vote determines where we give it.**

## OTG artifact

The minimum proposed artifact is:

```text
OURS TO GIVE

Amount: __________

This was mine to give.
Now it's ours to give.

Name — optional: ____________________
```

The name field remains explicitly optional. Absence of a name is an attribution choice rather than a failed identity submission.

## WHAT IS GIVEN

Submission gives the written OTG artifact to the collective. It contributes:

- the artifact;
- proposed amount;
- language and presentation;
- chosen attribution posture; and
- an opportunity another person may choose to carry.

The proposed amount is not yet capital received.

Acceptance means a user has deliberately selected the OTG and accepted responsibility for attempting to honor it. Acceptance does not make the writer a debtor.

Honoring supplies the stated capital. At that point:

> **Now it's ours to give.**

## PRESERVED MARKET MECHANICS

The correction preserves the useful parts of RFC 0002:

- users may shop OTGs;
- **Yeah, okay** remains acknowledgment rather than acceptance;
- acceptance remains voluntary and deliberate;
- writer and accepter choose attribution independently;
- collective human judgment determines which OTGs move forward;
- absurd offers remain inspectable without becoming received capital; and
- offered, acknowledged, accepted, honored, and credited evidence remains distinct.

An OTG for 1,000,000 from the Master of Disaster may increase the offered balance. It does not increase honored capital unless someone chooses to accept and honor it.

## RELATIONSHIP TO VOTING

Honored OTGs may capitalize the Capital Disposition Voting Test in RFC 0001 under separately declared terms.

- Ours to Give determines how an offered artifact becomes contributed capital.
- The voting test determines where received capital is given.

The capital is not framed as institutional property accumulated for retention:

> **The capital does not become ours to keep. It becomes ours to give.**

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

The joke search exposed a constitutional defect. A funny acronym was not worth language that placed contributors outside the system. Removing “Our Lovely System” from the creditor position did not fully solve the problem because debt remained the governing relation.

The operator's bodily-gift expression supplied the missing structure. The OTG pledge carries something from individual possession into collective stewardship without claiming that submission has already produced the stated capital.

The correction is therefore more than naming. It changes who the parties are and what kind of act they perform.

## CORRECTION

- **Accepted direction:** Ours to Give / OTG / offering and collective stewardship.
- RFC 0002 remains public as evidence of the superseded proposal.
- RFC 0003 carries the proposal forward under the corrected frame.
