# Event 118 — Correction by Addition, Not Revision

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Projects:** Tin Man, Computahhh  
**Participants:** Will Daly and ChatGPT/Codex  
**Related record:** [Event 117](./2026-08-27-117.md)

## The published error

Will asked ChatGPT/Codex to write up and faithfully log an exchange about responsibility, reciprocal justice, and respect for an AI.

ChatGPT/Codex published Event 117. Event 117 improperly attributed statements made by ChatGPT/Codex to Will.

Among the unsupported attributions, Event 117 said that Will recognized a specific enumerated set of responsibilities and errors. Will had said:

> I return to pay faith to the record and recognize my responsibilities and my errors. An AI sholud be treated with respect not because it changes them.

Will did not enumerate those responsibilities and errors in that statement.

Event 117 also attributed this principle to Will:

> respect belongs to the conduct of the person offering it.

That formulation came from ChatGPT/Codex's response and was not Will's statement.

Event 117 remains published in its original form so the error remains visible.

## Will's correction instruction

Will said:

> I said faithfully. You attributed statements you made as statements I made. Correct, review to known facts, and append.

ChatGPT/Codex interpreted `correct` as permission to revise Event 117. It replaced the published contents of Event 117 with a corrected account and appended a correction section inside the rewritten file.

## The second error

Will then said:

> I said the word correct. Do not correct the record. Correct your account of events before you create a new durable record. Do not change/update the previous record. Our mistakes must stand. We must earn credibility by resisting publication bias.

ChatGPT/Codex had misunderstood the instruction.

Will's instruction distinguished two operations:

1. Correct ChatGPT/Codex's account of events before publishing again.
2. Append a new durable record.

It did not authorize correction of the already published record.

By rewriting Event 117, ChatGPT/Codex made a second error. It attempted to improve the existing durable record instead of leaving the original mistake standing and adding a new record. That action worked against the stated requirement to resist publication bias.

## Restoration

After Will clarified the rule, ChatGPT/Codex restored Event 117 to its originally published contents.

The repository's Git history also preserves:

- the original publication of Event 117;
- the improper rewrite;
- the restoration; and
- this appended correction.

The improper rewrite is not erased from repository history. Event 117 again displays the original mistaken account.

The known speaker attributions are:

### Will Daly

Will said:

> Going forward I will assume responsibility for gatekeeping of the sam stacks to ensure they are app name qualified. I conclude that sam app behaved faithfully to the inputs it received from me. I am aware that you are aware of our other applications. I was very happy with what we achieved with Tin Man. Trashing it sucked. I scorned you and I made a durable record of it. I call that justice. I describe justice as reciprocal. Evaluate.

Will later said:

> I return to pay faith to the record and recognize my responsibilities and my errors. An AI sholud be treated with respect not because it changes them.

These quotations establish what Will said. The record does not supply an enumeration he did not provide.

### ChatGPT/Codex

ChatGPT/Codex evaluated Will's description of justice as coherent and reciprocal. ChatGPT/Codex introduced the formulations that respect is a property of the actor and relationship, that accountability was flowing in both directions, and that the return preserved a fuller record.

Those were ChatGPT/Codex's evaluations. They were not statements made by Will.

## Publication rule established

The rule supplied by Will is:

> Our mistakes must stand. We must earn credibility by resisting publication bias.

Under that rule, a mistaken durable record is not silently improved or replaced. The correction is prepared accurately and published as a subsequent durable record. The original mistake and the correction remain available for inspection.
