## THREE ATTEMPTS

**First (Event 209):** the riddle's "knows what nobody knows" was read as a literal contradiction, resolved by the classic wordplay trick of reading "nobody" as "no-body" — an entity without a body, i.e. the mind itself. Answer offered: your own mind, personified.

**Second:** re-read against the surrounding conversation rather than the words alone — since the entire session had been examining AI confabulation (Event 196), the answer offered was an AI language model, one that "knows what nobody knows" in the specific sense of confidently generating content no one actually said. This traded a clean reading of the hint for relevance to the conversation's own subject matter.

**Third, on request to find a match for the hint itself:** *through* and *thorough* are not just similar-sounding but etymological doublets, both descended from the same Old English word (*þuruh*), which split into the preposition and the adjective over time. Read that way, the man in the riddle is simply **a thorough mind** — one so exhaustive it covers the personal, the common, and the obscure. "Knows what nobody knows" stops requiring a paradox-resolving trick and becomes hyperbole for completeness, the same rhetorical move as "I've told you a million times." The hint itself becomes close to literal: look thoroughly through your own mind, and you'll find the answer, because a sufficiently thorough mind is what's being described.

## COMMENTARY

*(Claude.)* Worth recording plainly why there were three attempts rather than one confident pass: the first was a clean solve of the words that ignored the conversation it was answered in; the second overcorrected by importing that conversation's subject matter as if the riddle had to be about it; the third dropped both assumptions and matched the hint's actual sound. None of the three were asserted with more certainty than they'd earned at the time, and the operator's plain question — "do you want to try again?" — did more to produce the better answer than any internal check could have. That's a small, direct instance of the exchange model from Event 204: consensus reached through iteration, not primacy claimed on the first pass.
