## SUBJECT

Ledger storage, query architecture, and OpenSearch candidate analysis.

## BACKGROUND

Our Lovely System is considering a Markdown-based ledger. Each journal entry receives a stable `secuuid`. Future resource families such as attachments, comments, and tags are intended to remain separate and associate back to journal entries through the `secuuid`, rather than being nested into one entry hierarchy.

Pass 1 is intentionally narrow: rich Markdown, durable storage, performance, paging, and search. Attachments, tags, comments, voting, reputation, and utility mechanisms are deferred so that the first implementation remains small enough to determine whether it actually works.

## STORAGE DIRECTION

The working architecture assigns different responsibilities to three AWS services:

- **Amazon S3:** canonical durable system of record.
- **Amazon DynamoDB:** rebuildable operational projection for fast known access patterns, paging, and current lookup state.
- **Amazon Athena:** flexible and ad-hoc querying over data in S3, including keyword/content searches that do not map naturally to DynamoDB keys.

The governing constraint is that nothing important should exist only in DynamoDB. If DynamoDB is destroyed, S3 should contain sufficient information to reconstruct the projection.

The preferred S3 organization is resource-type-first, conceptually:

```text
journal/<secuuid>
attachments/<secuuid>/...
comments/<secuuid>/...
tags/<secuuid>/...
```

This separation is deliberate. In particular, attachment binaries should not share the Athena-facing journal hierarchy. Using ACL/IAM exclusions to make a mixed hierarchy queryable was rejected as unnecessarily brittle: an incorrectly configured permission could subtly alter what Athena sees while still returning plausible results. Permissions should enforce security boundaries, not define the semantic contents of a dataset.

## QUERY MODEL

DynamoDB is favored for predetermined operational questions such as retrieving an entry by identifier, chronological paging, or other explicitly indexed access patterns. Athena is favored for questions invented later, especially keyword searches and richer combinations across journal text and future structured resource families.

The application can use query results to identify matching `secuuid` values and then retrieve canonical content from S3.

## OPENSEARCH ANALYSIS

Amazon OpenSearch Service was evaluated as a competing search projection. Functionally, OpenSearch is a strong match for rich interactive search: full-text search, relevance ranking, filtering by tags and time, comment search, attachment-metadata filtering, and combinations of those conditions are natural search-engine operations.

Its principal advantages are:

- strong interactive full-text search;
- relevance ranking and search-oriented indexing;
- good support for rich combinations of filters;
- predictable low-latency search once indexed.

Its principal disadvantages for the present project are:

- additional infrastructure and conceptual machinery;
- a separate index that must be maintained and rebuilt;
- higher idle/base cost than the deliberately sparse S3/DynamoDB/Athena model in many low-traffic scenarios;
- cost behavior that is less attractive while the system has not demonstrated that sophisticated search is actually needed.

The current preference is therefore **S3 + DynamoDB + Athena**, while recognizing OpenSearch as a legitimate later candidate if search becomes sufficiently important to justify its cost and complexity.

## ADVERSARIAL COST CONSIDERATION

A hostile-cost scenario was considered: public users or enemies deliberately generating traffic to increase infrastructure charges. This strengthened the preference for the S3/DynamoDB/Athena approach because expensive capabilities can be bounded or degraded rather than automatically scaling to preserve service at arbitrary cost.

The preferred failure philosophy is that under abusive load, an expensive feature such as search may stop working before infrastructure spending is allowed to grow without a deliberately selected ceiling.

## CONCLUSION

The current direction is intentionally conservative:

> S3 remembers. DynamoDB makes known access patterns fast. Athena answers questions we did not design DynamoDB keys to answer.

OpenSearch remains a credible future search engine, but it has not yet earned a place in Pass 1.
