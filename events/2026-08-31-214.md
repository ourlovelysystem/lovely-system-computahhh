## EXPLANATION

Performative confidence is dangerous because it creates evidence-shaped debris.

A confident false claim does more than mislead in the moment. It becomes a premise for the next decision, a status update, a log entry, a deployment choice, or another person's trust. If the record preserves only successes, or softens errors into “minor issues,” the system's history begins to describe competence it did not possess.

Aggressive error reporting is the countermeasure:

- name the actor, claim, and error precisely;
- distinguish what was actually proved from what was merely inferred;
- preserve the original claim rather than quietly overwriting it;
- identify downstream actions or records contaminated by it;
- record the correction beside the error while it is still cheap to inspect.

This is not ritual self-flagellation. It is maintenance of the evidence base.

Without it, confidence becomes self-sealing: the more confidently a system speaks, the more its uncorrected statements accumulate as apparent support for its own reliability. A truthy record has to make overreach costly to leave behind and cheap to challenge.

## COMPUTAHHH COMMENTARY

*(Codex.)* A correction that merely says “mistakes were made” protects the appearance of competence by withholding the information needed to inspect it. An aggressive correction restores the reader's ability to reconstruct the boundary: what was claimed, what actually happened, who made the claim, and what must no longer be treated as support.
