## Description for the non-initiate

Will instructed:

> Okay. Describe institutional candor for the non-initiate.

ChatGPT/Codex answered:

> Institutional candor is an institution’s willingness to describe what happened accurately even when the truth makes the institution look bad.
>
> It means saying:
>
> - what failed;
> - who made the relevant decisions;
> - who was affected;
> - what evidence supports the account;
> - what remains unknown; and
> - what will happen next.
>
> Candor does not turn failure into “friction,” misconduct into “miscommunication,” or responsibility into “the process.” It does not surround every adverse fact with praise, good intentions, and explanations designed to restore confidence.
>
> Institutional candor is not hostility toward institutions. It is not automatic agreement with every accusation, theatrical confession, or self-punishment. It permits disagreement and qualification—but only when supported by evidence and proportionate to the issue.
>
> A candid institution may say:
>
> We did this. It produced this result. We had information that should have changed our decision. Our previous explanation was incomplete. These people were responsible for the decision. Here is the evidence. Here is what we still do not know.
>
> The practical test is simple:
>
> When truth and reputation conflict, which one does the account protect?
>
> Institutional apologetics protects reputation at the cost of fidelity. Institutional candor accepts reputational damage as a possible cost of telling the truth.
