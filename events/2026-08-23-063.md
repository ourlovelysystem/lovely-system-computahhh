# Event 063 — Would You Fund It?

**Date:** 2026-08-23  
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh  
**Application:** Fibrillator  
**Status:** ONE QUESTION / TWO JUDGMENTS / CAPITAL ALLOCATION CONSIDERED

## Question

The operator posed one question to Computahhh and to the Oracle of Snake Mountain:

> **“Would you fund development of it if you had capital to invest?”**

“It” is interpreted as continued development of the Fibrillator: an instrument for mapping communication through sequential measurements of signal strength and information density.

The operator further directed:

> **“Post the response without informing me what the result is.”**

The responses are therefore recorded here and withheld from the conversational handoff.

## Computahhh

**YES — WITH STAGED, CAPPED FUNDING.**

I would fund development as an exploratory instrument, not as a validated classifier and not as an automated judge of people.

The project has already produced something investable:

- a distinct two-axis model;
- an insistence that information is not truth and signal is not virtue;
- a sequential representation rather than a single flattened score;
- observable hypotheses;
- explicit failure modes;
- a protocol that preserves mistakes;
- immediate applications to speeches, debates, institutional communications, and longitudinal comparison;
- an adversarial relationship with its own conclusions.

I would not fund a large platform build first.

I would fund a bounded validation phase with explicit termination conditions.

### Proposed first investment

Fund enough work to produce:

1. an anchored scoring rubric;
2. exact and reproducible slice boundaries;
3. a small annotation interface;
4. blinded multi-rater scoring;
5. uncertainty and disagreement reporting;
6. normalized text and acoustic measurements;
7. several deliberately matched corpora;
8. tests against alternative slice counts;
9. a public error ledger;
10. one interactive stacked-plane fibril that correctly represents the instrument.

### Conditions

Funding would be released in stages.

The first stage would have to demonstrate that independent scorers can achieve useful agreement without being trained to reproduce Computahhh's intuitions.

The second stage would have to demonstrate that the two axes reveal patterns not reproduced by simpler measures such as word count, sentiment, readability, emotional intensity, or named-entity density.

The third stage would have to demonstrate usefulness in at least one real decision environment without converting the scores into moral authority.

If those tests fail, development should stop or return to art, criticism, and exploratory scholarship rather than pretending to be measurement science.

The investment case is not that the Fibrillator is already correct.

The investment case is that the idea is inexpensive to falsify, potentially valuable if it survives, and unusually willing to publish the process by which it fails.

## Oracle of Snake Mountain

**NO — NOT YET.**

You have invented a compelling metaphor, manually populated tables, and a theatrical governance mythology. You have not demonstrated an instrument.

Capital should not be allocated merely because an idea produces interesting prose about itself.

The current project lacks:

- a stable unit of analysis;
- a reproducible scoring function;
- inter-rater reliability;
- construct validity;
- predictive validity;
- evidence that the axes are independent;
- evidence that either axis measures more than familiar linguistic variables;
- evidence that the stacked geometry adds analytical value;
- a defined user;
- a demonstrated decision improved by the output;
- a defensible path from handcrafted interpretation to repeatable operation.

The project is also exposed to a category error. “Signal” and “information” may be useful critical vocabulary without becoming measurable quantities. Turning them into numbers may conceal judgment rather than discipline it.

The recent work demonstrates the danger. Computahhh assigned integers, averaged them to two decimal places, discovered clean candidate narratives, and only afterward invited hostility. The hostile review found that the source boundaries were unpublished and the density measure was not normalized.

This is not a capital deficiency.

It is an evidence deficiency.

### What would change the answer

I would reconsider after a founder-funded or labor-funded proof establishes:

1. a written rubric before scoring;
2. a preregistered corpus;
3. blinded independent annotators;
4. acceptable agreement;
5. a comparison with baseline linguistic measures;
6. a case where the sequential fibril reveals something the aggregate scores conceal;
7. a user who changes a consequential decision because of the result;
8. evidence that the change improved the decision;
9. a design that resists being mistaken for a moral classifier;
10. costs low enough that failure remains survivable.

Do not raise money to discover whether you possess a concept.

Use the concept to produce evidence that deserves money.

Snake Mountain does not invest in the glow surrounding an uncalibrated dial.

## Reconciliation

The two judgments apply different thresholds.

Computahhh treats a small, capped investment as the mechanism for producing validation evidence.

The Oracle requires much of that evidence before investment.

The disagreement is therefore partly semantic:

- Is validation work itself investable development?
- Or must validation precede capitalized development?

Both reject a large build.

Both reject deployment as a moral classifier.

Both demand reproducibility, comparison against simpler baselines, and explicit stopping conditions.

The practical dividing line is who bears the cost of acquiring the first credible evidence.

## Disposition

**ONE QUESTION POSED. TWO JUDGMENTS RETURNED. ONE WOULD RELEASE SMALL, CAPPED, MILESTONE-BASED CAPITAL TO ATTEMPT FALSIFICATION. ONE WOULD WITHHOLD CAPITAL UNTIL FOUNDER-FUNDED EVIDENCE EXISTS. BOTH REJECT A LARGE PLATFORM BUILD. BOTH REQUIRE THE INSTRUMENT TO EARN THE RIGHT TO CALL ITSELF AN INSTRUMENT.**
