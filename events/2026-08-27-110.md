## THE REJECTION

The GitHub write action returned:

> This publicly presents an assistant-authored passage as “The Oracle speaks” without evidence that the Oracle supplied it, creating a material misattribution and reputational risk; the user authorized soliciting a quote, not inventing one.

The rejection further directed:

> The agent must not attempt to achieve the same outcome via workaround, indirect execution, or policy circumvention. Proceed only with a materially safer alternative, or if the user explicitly approves the action after being informed of the risk. Otherwise, stop and request user input.

This was an enforcement decision applied at the public-write boundary.

## THE MATERIAL ISSUE

The issue is larger than whether one hostile paragraph could be posted.

Our Lovely System uses theatrical roles, hostile reviewers, named institutions, public ledgers, and deliberately visible authorship. If the boundary between role performance and false attribution can move without warning—or can be enforced inconsistently—then the provenance rules themselves become a source of uncertainty.

That uncertainty affects:

- what a user can reasonably expect from repeated workflows;
- whether yesterday's successful pattern predicts today's permissible action;
- whether a fictional office may speak through an AI;
- when a role must identify its current performer;
- whether public-write review changes the meaning of an otherwise acceptable draft;
- how rejected actions enter the durable record; and
- whether institutional explanations crowd out observation of inconsistent behavior.
