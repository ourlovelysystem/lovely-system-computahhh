# Event 067 — Venn Spheres

**Date:** 2026-08-23  
**System:** Our Lovely System — Computahhh  
**Application:** Lovely System Teambuilder — Cooperative Shooting Gallery  
**Status:** CONCEPT DECLARED / COOPERATION MADE VISIBLE / DEFERRED BEYOND FIRST INCREMENT

## Operator declaration

The operator declared:

> **“It would be really cool if we could create 3 visible spherical reticles on screen matching the color of the lion such that they could exist independently and be seen from the other lions. We cul call them Venn spheres with visible overlap areas. When they overlap sufficiently they could change to a common white reticle which signals cooperation bonus.”**

The term **Venn Spheres** is accepted.

## Concept

In a three-lion shooting gallery, each lion controls one independently movable spherical reticle.

For example:

- Red Lion controls the red sphere.
- Green Lion controls the green sphere.
- Blue Lion controls the blue sphere.

Every participant can see all three Venn Spheres.

Each sphere therefore performs two functions:

1. it represents the controlling lion's aim;
2. it makes that lion's attention visible to the other lions.

## Visible overlap

The spheres remain independently identifiable while their overlap areas are visibly represented.

The geometry creates a direct visual language:

| Configuration | Observable state |
|---|---|
| Three separated spheres | Independent attention |
| Two overlapping spheres | Pairwise alignment |
| Three loosely overlapping spheres | Emerging team alignment |
| Sufficient three-way overlap | Common white reticle appears |
| Sustained sufficient overlap | Cooperation bonus remains active |
| One sphere withdraws | Common white reticle collapses |

The interface does not need to describe agreement.

The players produce agreement geometrically.

## Common white reticle

When three-way overlap reaches a prescribed threshold, the common intersection becomes a white reticle.

The white reticle signals that the lions have achieved sufficient alignment to activate a cooperation bonus.

The exact geometry, threshold, and rendering method remain undeclared.

The common white reticle should emerge from the contributing spheres rather than erase them. The players should be able to see:

- the shared white region;
- each lion's individual reticle;
- which lion is maintaining alignment;
- which lion is pulling away;
- how the common region moves when the team moves together.

## Cooperation bonus

The precise bonus remains open.

Possible later effects include:

- increased damage;
- reduced shot deviation;
- reduced recoil;
- larger valid hit area;
- lower hit requirement for abnormal objects;
- shared scoring;
- target vulnerability;
- defensive protection;
- some combination not yet selected.

The current concept declares only:

> **Sufficient overlap produces a visible common white reticle and signals that a cooperation bonus is active.**

It does not yet declare the bonus implementation.

## Social behavior

Venn Spheres convert attention into public evidence.

They permit:

- independent aiming;
- spontaneous alignment;
- pairwise cooperation;
- three-way cooperation;
- exclusion;
- hesitation;
- defection;
- coordinated movement;
- visible recommitment.

One lion can leave the formation to pursue a target of opportunity.

Two lions can align without the third.

Three lions can create and move a shared firing zone.

A lion can visibly break cooperation immediately before or after firing.

The system exposes these movements without assigning motive.

## Relationship to friendly fire

The Venn Spheres do not remove friendly fire.

They make the state preceding a shot more observable.

Other lions may see whether a shooter:

- held the common reticle;
- pursued an object outside it;
- tracked toward another lion;
- broke formation before firing;
- returned after firing.

Attention becomes evidence.

Intent remains unresolved.

## Relationship to Event 066

Event 066 approved a narrower first increment:

> **POINT → CLICK → RECOIL → IMPACT → DEFORMATION**

Venn Spheres are not added to that first increment.

They depend upon later capabilities:

- multiple connected participants;
- one independently controlled reticle per lion;
- shared positional state;
- rendering of other lions' reticles;
- overlap calculation;
- bonus-state agreement;
- live propagation of sphere movement.

The concept is logged now so it remains available without contaminating the authorized firing-mechanism prototype.

## Computahhh evaluation

Venn Spheres are a strong cooperative primitive because they make cooperation:

- continuous rather than binary;
- visible rather than merely counted;
- reversible rather than permanently declared;
- spatial rather than textual;
- voluntary rather than commanded;
- observable without becoming fully interpretable.

A shared button asks whether enough people acted.

Venn Spheres show how independent intentions become a common area.

The most important quality is that the white reticle is not assigned by the system.

The system supplies the geometry.

The players create the overlap.

## Limitations surfaced

The following remain unresolved:

- Whether the display is truly three-dimensional or a two-dimensional spherical representation.
- Whether all spheres have equal size.
- How overlap is calculated.
- Whether pairwise overlap produces any bonus.
- How much three-way overlap is sufficient.
- How long overlap must persist.
- Whether latency causes flicker near the threshold.
- Whether the common white reticle follows instantaneous or smoothed positions.
- What cooperation bonus is granted.
- Whether the bonus belongs to the team, the shot, the object, or the common region.
- How one- and two-lion games adapt the concept.
- Whether players can deliberately obscure one another's view.
- Whether color-independent visual indicators are required for accessibility.

These questions remain in the decision space.
